# NanoHost Transport

This specification owns the NanoCore-to-NanoHost transport session and its nested Sandbox carriage projection: physical listeners and protocol versions, NanoHost transport credentials and server TLS trust, HTTP/2 stream envelopes and flow-control reservations, fixed NanoHost effect operations and private Harness control carriage, liveness and outage timers, predecessor fencing, and failure recovery.

## Scope

### Owns

- The single authoritative NanoHost-initiated HTTP/2 transport connection between NanoHost and NanoCore, its listener separation, server-authenticated TLS trust, and shared transport revision check.
- The NanoHost transport credential class (`nanohost-transport`), enrollment and two-slot (`A`/`B`) delivery, overlap rotation, revocation, decommission, connection-generation allocation, and per-Turn route-family token separation.
- Outer and nested HTTP/2 stream reservations, receive windows, per-family `DATA` ceilings, diagnostic trace-header and deadline-header boundaries, and single-file effect carriage.
- The 12 fixed NanoHost effect operation pairs, the `image.build/input` subpath, private `/worker-control/harness/poll` and `/worker-control/harness/result` carriage for the seven Harness operations, liveness and outage timers, and transport failure/reconnect fencing.
- Route-family separation and independent carriage boundaries across shared physical HTTP/2 sessions.

### Does Not Own

- Runtime Epoch topology, compatibility keys, Sandbox/Harness/AgentSession residency lifecycle, Image Store, distribution archives, operator inspection, forensic Epoch Invalidation Reports, Host-to-Gateway gRPC operations, the stock `ForwardTcp`/`RelayOpen`/`ConnectSupervisor`/`RelayStream` bridge realization, Linux namespace containment, and stock OpenShell release qualification in `docs/specs/20260802-nanohost_runtime_and_transport.md`.
- Durable execution intent/outcome transactions, reservation commit gaps, and journal step ownership in `docs/specs/20261019-durable_execution_and_control_store.md`.
- Worker-control message schemas and Turn reliability in `docs/specs/20260703-worker_control_protocol.md`, `docs/specs/20260629-worker_runtime_communication_model.md`, and `docs/specs/20260531-worker_turn_reliability_envelope.md`.
- Workspace large-data staging and collection payloads in `docs/specs/20260801-nanohost_workspace_data_boundary.md`, Vault secret injection in `docs/specs/20260703-vault_secret_injection.md`, and LLM Gateway/capability semantics in their respective owners.

## Parties And Listeners

NanoHost initiates and owns the physical transport connection as the HTTP/2 client, and NanoCore accepts it on the dedicated native HTTP/2 listener selected by `nanohost.bind` (`apps/nanocore/src/index.ts`). At steady state, NanoCore and the configured NanoHost MUST have exactly one authoritative authenticated physical transport connection carrying all declared readiness, fixed NanoHost-effect, worker-control, inference, and capability routes under the route-family separation rules in [Envelope](#envelope) and fencing any predecessor connection generation before replacement.

NanoCore operates two distinct listeners on separate local TCP ports (sharing one port across interface addresses is not supported):

- **Ordinary App listener (`server.bind`)**: Serves HTTP/1.1 and SSE (over TLS HTTPS when configured) for public App API, authentication, Gateway, diagnostics, and SSE routes, and rejects `/api/nanohost/transport/*` and the nested-session family prefixes (`/worker-control/*`, `/inference/*`, `/capabilities/*`) with `404`.
- **Dedicated NanoHost listener (`nanohost.bind`)**: Serves native HTTP/2 only (secure `h2` over TLS when configured, or cleartext HTTP/2 only on exact same-host loopback) and MUST accept only `/api/nanohost/transport/*` and the three nested-session family prefixes `/worker-control/*`, `/inference/*`, and `/capabilities/*`, rejecting every public App API, authentication, Gateway, diagnostics, and SSE path. A reverse proxy, Caddy, browser, ordinary fetch client, HTTP/1.1 request, or synthetic application request cannot enter or stand in for this listener. The configured `rendezvousUrl` is the origin dialed by NanoHost, while `nanohost.bind` is the local server bind and may differ only through an explicitly owned deployment mapping. NanoCore MUST NOT initiate a parallel management connection into the NanoHost network, and neither party may fall back to SSH, an operator or external Gateway forward, a sandbox-direct endpoint, or a second route-specific connection.

Inside each active Sandbox (`docs/specs/20260802-nanohost_runtime_and_transport.md`), Sandbox Integration binds two distinct fixed loopback endpoints before emitting its startup marker (`packages/worker-shim/src/integration-client.ts`): loopback TCP port `17891` for the Supervisor raw bridge stream carrying nested standard HTTP/2 (with Integration as the HTTP/2 client and NanoHost as the HTTP/2 server), and loopback TCP port `17892` for the native Agent HTTP/1 listener, which admits native `POST` requests only after the nested HTTP/2 session is ready and never acts as a second outer Host connection.

Every non-loopback NanoCore endpoint—including an endpoint reached over a container bridge, host LAN, overlay, tunnel, or remote host—MUST use server-authenticated TLS validated against normal platform roots or an explicitly configured non-secret CA reference, offering only `h2` via ALPN within the existing startup and readiness hard bounds (certificate pinning and mTLS are not used, and no verification bypass or plaintext fallback is permitted). Exact same-host loopback MAY use plaintext HTTP/2 under local trust rules; privately routed bridge, LAN, overlay, tunnel, and remote addresses are non-loopback and require TLS. A deployment MAY use an upstream TLS terminator only when explicitly declared and when NanoHost validates the external endpoint presented by that terminator; trust termination MUST NOT silently move to an undeclared internal plaintext hop visible to NanoHost.

## Identity And Credentials

This specification owns the NanoHost transport credential class (`nanohost-transport`) and its issuance, enrollment, secret delivery, storage, rotation, revocation, Audit, decommission, and trust rules (`packages/app-api-schemas/src/nanohost.ts`, `apps/nanocore/src/auth/`, `apps/nanohost/src/credential_slots.rs`). Human access tokens (`docs/specs/20260704-remote_auth_credential_bootstrap.md`), `server-admin` tokens, AuthSessions, worker-control/inference/capability route tokens, OpenShell Gateway credentials, provider credentials, and Vault material retain their separate lifecycles and owners.

### Configured Identity And Bearer Token

- **Configured NanoHost identity**: Exactly one deployment-scoped `IntegrationIdentity` (using its integration `ActorRef` for governed attribution) is accepted as the execution target in the current profile; it is not a user, administrator, Worker, AgentSession, Gateway, Sandbox, credential, attempt, or product record. Reconfiguration replaces the accepted identity rather than adding a fleet member, and any missing, stale, unknown, revoked, or conflicting identity, credential, trust, runtime, lineage, or proof fails closed before work admission, route traffic, readiness, or connection replacement.
- **Dedicated bearer Token**: V1 authenticates the NanoCore-to-NanoHost transport using one dedicated bearer NanoHost Token over server-authenticated TLS (never a `server-admin` token or mTLS). The Token is a Core `Token` projection owned by the configured `IntegrationIdentity` with closed type and scope `nanohost-transport`, bound to one NanoHost identity and one deployment. Product, administration, worker-control, inference, capability, Gateway, provider, and Vault routes MUST reject the NanoHost Token, and the NanoHost transport MUST reject every other credential class.
- **Secret format, storage, and redaction**: The raw secret reuses the `okt_` opaque-secret primitive with at least `256` bits (`32` bytes) of CSPRNG entropy encoded as one URL-safe value, with dedicated hash-only Core `Token` storage separate from human `openkit_access_tokens`. As a named existing-form exception to [Protocol Identifiers And Enums](20261021-protocol_identifiers_and_enums.md#ids), NanoCore hashes the `okt_` secret string through `hashOpenKitAccessTokenSecret` (`apps/nanocore/src/auth/access-token.ts`) and stores only that one-way `sha256` hash (`64` lowercase hex characters) and non-secret metadata (`id`, owner NanoHost identity, `nanohost-transport` type/scope, deployment, issuance/expiry, status, rotation lineage, responsible `server-admin` actor, and redacted last-use summary), verifies hashes in constant time, and never stores, recovers, returns, or exposes the raw secret after delivery. Secret scanners and redaction filters treat every `okt_` value as credential material; raw tokens and hashes MUST NOT appear in stdout, stderr, argv, environment dumps, logs, documentation, diagnostics, Artifacts, AEPs, product records, Audit records, or generated examples.

### Enrollment, Two-Slot Storage, Rotation, And Revocation

- **Explicit enrollment and reactivation**: Explicit `server-admin` enrollment atomically creates the configured `IntegrationIdentity` and first `nanohost-transport` Token or leaves neither active. When the exact configured identity and deployment already match one retained decommissioned identity, explicit re-enrollment reactivates that same durable identity (preserving original creation time and redacted Token/actor/Audit history while clearing decommissioned status/time) and issues one fresh Token while leaving every prior Token revoked; active, cross-identity, cross-deployment, duplicate, or conflicting lineage fails closed without mutation.
- **Two-slot delivery (`A` and `B`)**: The enrollment request explicitly selects one of the two declared execution-host slots (`A` or `B`, with `A` as the current default at first issuance while either named slot is valid). Before writing, both paired destination paths (secret and companion) for the target slot MUST be proved absent and created without overwrite; the operation writes the raw secret and non-secret companion once and returns only redacted identity, token-reference, and slot-result metadata. Each slot is a root- or service-owned OS credential entry or `0600` file owned by the NanoHost service account outside every mutable Runtime Epoch and inside the deployment boundary (remote installation may use operator-authorized installation-time SSH stdin or a declared platform secret-provisioning facility only when proving non-echoing named sink delivery, with no SSH runtime control or fallback except the pinned opaque stock-internal single-file helper relay).
- **Slot isolation and runtime selection**: Execution-host credential-sink exclusions provide host-to-Sandbox isolation only, not co-resident AgentSession isolation. Stable slot locations `A` and `B` are deployment configuration outside Epoch identity, so installing, rotating, or clearing slot contents never invalidates a healthy Epoch or interrupts a Session. Each slot's non-secret companion carries only Token ID, issuance generation, owning NanoHost identity, and deployment (no secret or hash). The companion is written only by the enrollment or rotation operation that writes its slot and has the same ownership and mode requirements as that slot (`#725`). At each connection attempt, NanoHost rereads both slots and presents only the usable slot whose companion declares the higher unique issuance generation for the configured identity and deployment, without caching across connections, searching any other file/env/process/Gateway/legacy location, or inferring generation from mtime, filename, or length. In steady state one slot is usable (an aborted successor is cleared); companion metadata is an ordering hint only, malformed/missing/wrong-identity/wrong-deployment/equal-generation-ambiguous slots are treated as empty, and when both slots are empty or ambiguous NanoHost stays non-ready without a credential-probing loop. Gateway, backend, Sandboxes, Integration, native children, diagnostic users, and normal operator commands MUST NOT read either slot, and configuration/AEP carry only non-secret references.
- **Overlap rotation**: Explicit `server-admin` rotation records predecessor-successor lineage and a bounded overlap deadline (current default `24 hours` / `86,400 s`, configurable down to `0`, with no post-expiry grace). Rotation writes the successor exclusively to the inactive slot without overwriting the predecessor or changing configuration or Epoch state. The predecessor remains the sole authoritative credential until a candidate connection authenticated by the installed successor wins a connection-generation fence over the predecessor, which marks the predecessor `rotated` or `revoked` (incapable of reconnecting) and allows explicit cleanup of the superseded slot. Before cutover, explicit `server-admin` abort may revoke and clear the successor while keeping the predecessor authoritative, or issue a fresh successor into the same inactive slot; rotated, revoked, or expired slots are treated as empty and never used as fallback. Token lineage, status, overlap deadline, and connection generation form the complete rotation state, with no credential recovery coordinator and never two authoritative sessions.
- **Revocation and Audit**: Revocation immediately denies the Token at the auth layer, fences the current authoritative session, stops new admission, and marks the Host non-ready. Enrollment, issuance, sink result, rotation, cutover/abort, revocation, expiry fencing, and decommission emit redacted Audit records naming NanoHost identity, Token/lineage IDs, responsible `server-admin` `ActorRef`, deployment, action, result, and timestamp, with no raw token, hash, sink contents, private path, TLS key material, or credential fragment.

### Connection Generations And Route/Session Credentials

- **Connection-generation allocation**: Connection admission authenticates NanoHost identity, deployment, credential, and protocol against the exact physical connection before the transport-session authority runs one checked SQLite transaction allocating generation `1` (first connection) or the durable high-water `connection_generation` plus `1`, persisting it as non-ready and unfenced before process-local binding (NanoHost never chooses a generation). Pre-commit failures, unauthenticated/stale requests, concurrent losers, repeated admissions on the same physical connection, and integer overflow do not advance the generation; post-commit binding failure consumes the allocated generation and leaves it durably non-ready and fenced.
- **Per-Turn route tokens**: Every active Turn mints three independent `32`-byte (`43`-character base64url) raw tokens—one each for `/worker-control/*`, `/inference/*`, and `/capabilities/*`—distinct from one another, from the NanoHost Token, and from `sandboxBindingRef`. As a named existing-form exception to [Protocol Identifiers And Enums](20261021-protocol_identifiers_and_enums.md#ids), the scheduler attempt owner persists only three nullable lowercase `sha256` (`64`-hex) projections per live attempt computed over the decoded base64url token bytes through `hashWorkerRouteToken` (`apps/nanocore/src/runtime/worker-control-gateway.ts`), never raw tokens, and each route family rejects the other two families' tokens and sibling Session tokens. Raw route tokens live only in memory in NanoCore, NanoHost during active operation carriage, and that Turn's AgentSession-local route boundary (never in Harness-global env, Sandbox-wide files, native argv, or another Session). Across a NanoCore restart, Core reconstructs active Turn bindings from the attempt-owned hashes and current AEP/lineage while surviving NanoHost and Session-local boundaries keep the same raw tokens without reissuance or relaunch; terminal, stale, fenced, or releasing lineage revokes all three bindings (preserving only exact accepted `final_status` replay idempotency).
- **Session loopback and Vault runtime environment credentials**: At `session.open`, Sandbox Integration mints one inference and one capability loopback credential pair delivered only to the native Agent child, while `turn.start` delivers per-Turn route tokens to Integration only; Integration maps arriving native loopback requests to the currently bound Turn's route token, refuses requests when no Turn is bound, drains in-flight requests at the Turn barrier, and destroys the loopback pair at `session.close` (`docs/specs/20260703-worker_agent_capability.md`). Provider and Vault secrets remain with their owners (`docs/specs/20260703-vault_secret_injection.md`): AEP carries only non-secret references and local bindings, while session-static `runtimeEnvironment` (delivered on `session.open` in memory only after durable command recording, never persisted in commands/AEP/logs or refreshed on later Turns) carries at most `128` NUL-free non-empty entries (`<= 64 KiB` per value) matching exact AEP declarations, rejecting protected control/bootstrap/adapter names and public `nativeEnvironment` collisions, entering only the current native child environment and exact-value redaction set, and recording a sink receipt only after native-start acknowledgement (missing/invalid material refuses startup, and lost acknowledgement stays `unknown`).

### Owner-Local Transport Recovery

The NanoHost extension retains integration identities, runtime targets, transport verifiers, connection generations and native Worker-control evidence. The kernel retains attempt grants and revocation. A replacement transport authenticates its own connection and fences the predecessor generation before readiness. Re-adopting an existing Worker also requires proof of its original process, attempt and generation under a live kernel grant. A valid ledger or an old verifier alone establishes no current binding. If the transport store is missing, unsupported or skewed, refuse dependent routes while keeping kernel revocation available. After an uncertain restore, fence or reauthorize affected credentials before admitting their dependent effects. Route verification uses the current generation and original attempt. Revoke all actual grants before successor readiness. [Storage Layout](20260703-storage_layout_record_ownership.md#server-database-allowlist) owns physical placement. The wire boundary keeps its existing version markers.

## Envelope

Both the outer NanoHost-to-NanoCore HTTP/2 session and the nested Sandbox-Integration-to-NanoHost HTTP/2 session enforce explicit per-family stream reservations, byte/flow-control accounting, worker-control liveness, and bridge re-establishment bounds (`apps/nanohost/src/sandbox_bridge.rs`, `apps/nanohost/src/nanocore_session.rs`, `packages/worker-shim/src/integration-client.ts`):

- **Physical admission and TLS byte framing**: The dedicated NanoCore HTTP/2 server assigns each accepted physical connection an opaque, non-serializable process-local identity supplied only through server connection context; the admission request body is `{}` with no caller-selected or proxy-supplied connection context. The TLS byte adapter preserves every encrypted socket byte until `rustls` consumes it and every plaintext byte until HTTP/2 consumes it, keeping application bytes and connection lifetime invariant under arbitrary socket, TLS record, or HTTP/2 frame fragmentation and coalescing.
- **Route-family separation and nested-to-outer projection**: Shared physical carriage never merges route tokens, scopes, limits, retries, failure meanings, usage, Audit, or protocol ownership across route families; it preserves six logical route boundaries—NanoHost control/readiness, fixed file-data/build-input, private `/worker-control/harness/poll` and `result`, per-Turn `/worker-control/*`, `/inference/*`, and `/capabilities/*`—each keeping its own authentication, exact lineage binding, payload ceiling, timeout, retry budget, usage/Audit mapping, and terminal meaning (supporting conversation-context isolation without claiming co-resident security or adjudication isolation against a compromised shared Harness). NanoHost terminates the nested Sandbox HTTP/2 server side and projects accepted streams onto the outer HTTP/2 connection as a bounded three-namespace bridge (`/worker-control/*`, `/inference/*`, `/capabilities/*`), rejecting `CONNECT`, caller-selected origins, absolute URLs, dynamic route registration, or fourth namespaces, and preserving path, body, streaming, cancellation, auth, lineage, order, and failure without synthesizing, retrying, reordering, or terminalizing requests.
- **No authority or response caching**: NanoHost MUST NOT cache AEPs, policy/capability/permission grants, leases, or freshness state (acting only on an immutable deadline-bounded AEP grant without refresh or fallback), and MUST NOT cache inference responses, capability results, or provider outputs because usage, metering, and Audit belong to NanoCore.
- **Stream permits, receive windows, and chunking**:
  - **Per-family stream reservation**: Open streams are counted per route family; reserved worker-control slots are never borrowed by other families. A permit covers the full request and response exchange (including response body) and is released on exchange completion or termination; a family at its stream ceiling receives an immediate HTTP `429` transport refusal before semantic dispatch without silent queueing.
  - **Explicit flow-control and `DATA` accounting**: Both sessions configure explicit per-stream initial receive windows, release window capacity only as the application consumes bytes, enforce per-family in-flight `DATA` ceilings (while semantic request-collection bounds remain distinct: worker-control `1 MiB`, native inference `16 MiB` at Integration and `2 MiB` ordinary body check at Core, native capability `512 KiB`, and Harness control `64 KiB`), and bound each forwarded inference stream write under the numeric bounds in [Stream, Window, And Liveness Envelope](#stream-window-and-liveness-envelope) so bursts cannot monopolize the connection window. The reserved worker-control window floor equals the reserved worker-control stream count multiplied by the per-stream initial window (`largest permitted worker-control payload` basis).
  - **Outer Host reservations and data exclusion**: On the outer connection, the independent control/readiness and fixed file-data stream reservations in [Stream, Window, And Liveness Envelope](#stream-window-and-liveness-envelope) bound file-data carriage across `reference.import`, `file.export`, `workspace.collect` result, and `image.build/input`. Large Workspace trees, Artifacts, repository packs, model files, and image archives never ride semantic control, readiness, worker-control, inference, or capability routes (`docs/specs/20260801-nanohost_workspace_data_boundary.md`), and data paths never carry control commands, route credentials, readiness, attempt ownership, or terminal-status authority.
- **Command JSON, descriptive readers, and trace/deadline headers**:
  - Every non-import `200` effect command body is bounded operation-specific JSON within the `512 KiB` generic control ceiling, carrying a lowercase `64`-hex `requestId` and closed operation fields (`workspace.collect` admits additive members under `docs/specs/20260801-nanohost_workspace_data_boundary.md`, and `image.build` carries `dockerfileByteLength` `1..268,435,456` as metadata while omitting Dockerfile bytes).
  - Under [Contract Evolution](../core/contract-evolution.md#closed-core-open-extension), readiness, admission, Harness polls, fixed-effect polls, and effect results are descriptive on read (stripping inert additions only after digest verification while keeping physical-connection authority, generation, identity, request correlation, capacity/storage lineage, closed dispositions, required proofs, and changed-core replay rejection strict), whereas Core-to-NanoHost effect commands, compiled policy, and storage attachment instructions remain exact.
  - Optional W3C `traceparent` and `tracestate` headers are validated as diagnostic context only (`docs/specs/20260731-operational_telemetry_standardization.md#nanohost-correlation`): the execution journal in `docs/specs/20261019-durable_execution_and_control_store.md` is the sole durable trace/span correlation owner, and no trace field enters a NanoHost body, canonical record, or command/result fingerprint. Propagated deadline metadata is distinct control metadata: an operation requiring an authenticated transport deadline header MUST verify that it matches or narrows the stored operation allowance and refuse missing or extended values while keeping JSON and file fingerprints unchanged (#793).

## Operations

### Transport Readiness

The sole transport readiness report is `POST /api/nanohost/transport/session/readiness` on the authoritative native HTTP/2 connection (`apps/nanohost/src/nanocore_session.rs`, `apps/nanocore/src/runtime/nanohost-session-dispatch.ts`):

- NanoHost sends `{ "physicalEpoch": "<64 lowercase hex characters>" }` only after `EpochCoordinator::start` succeeds and physical connection admission returns `role=authoritative` and `mayCarryWork=true`, awaiting the durable empty `204` response before issuing any effect poll (a candidate sends no readiness report, and any non-`204`, non-empty, malformed, cancelled, or lost response fails the outer session without polling).
- NanoCore obtains the physical connection from accepted server context, derives its bound generation from the transport-session authority, derives configured target identity and deployment from the allocated `RuntimeTarget`, sets `predecessorFenced=true` and server-clock `observedAt`, and durably projects `ready=true` and the coordinator's fresh-empty `physicalEpoch` witness before returning `204`. Readiness never creates a `RuntimeTarget`, allocates or advances a generation, or grants/releases Turn capacity, and rejects malformed/missing fields, a changed `physicalEpoch` within an already-ready generation, non-native/synthetic requests, candidates, closed/fenced predecessors, or mismatched lineage without mutation.

### Twelve Fixed NanoHost Effect Pairs And Subpaths

The closed NanoHost effect vocabulary has exactly `12` operation pairs defined by `EFFECT_PATHS` in `apps/nanohost/src/nanocore_session.rs` and `NanoHostEffectOperation` in `apps/nanocore/src/runtime/nanohost-session-dispatch.ts` (see [Protocol Identifiers And Enums](20261021-protocol_identifiers_and_enums.md#canonical-enums)):

- **Command and result paths**: Each `<operation>` uses two private HTTP/2 paths on the authoritative connection: `POST /api/nanohost/transport/effects/<operation>` (poll body `{}` returning `204` when none is pending or `200` with one command) and `POST /api/nanohost/transport/effects/<operation>/result`. `attempt-session.cleanup` is a Core composition of `bridge.close` and `sandbox.delete`, not a wire operation. The sole additional subpath is `POST /api/nanohost/transport/effects/image.build/input` for an accepted pending `image.build`. NanoHost cycles the 12 poll paths fairly with at most one effect-control request open at a time.
- **Deterministic `requestId` and result matching**: As a named existing-form exception to [Protocol Identifiers And Enums](20261021-protocol_identifiers_and_enums.md#ids), for mutating operations `createNanoHostEffectRequest` (`apps/nanocore/src/runtime/nanohost-effect-identity.ts`) deterministically derives the lowercase `64`-hex `requestId` as `SHA-256` over the `stableNanoHostEffectJson` bytes of `{backendSessionId, input: identityInput, leaseId: attemptId, operation, packageSnapshotId}` (recursively ordering object keys with `compareCodeUnits`, preserving array order, using `JSON.stringify` leaves with `undefined` mapped to `null`, and retaining that function's selected identity inputs and private-field exclusions—excluding `body`, `source`, `sha256`, and `byteLength` on `runtime-credential` / `runtime-credential-replace` `reference.import` and `checkValues` on `workspace.collect`—alongside exact Dockerfile bytes before wire projection removes them, AgentSession/Turn/slot/path/presence/barrier lineage for terminal export, producing package and submission `requestId` for active Artifact export, or immutable candidate Artifact ID/version for environment-preparation `image.acquire` / `image.build`); NanoHost never selects `requestId`, dispatch creates no durable queue or journal, and current administrator authority is checked at preparation dispatch and candidate application while already dispatched effects can settle after revocation. NanoCore accepts a result only on the matching fixed path and current authoritative connection for the expected pending transition and `requestId`, rejecting wrong candidates, generations, operations, or changed duplicates.
- **Retained-result recovery, preparation deferral, and delete discard**:
  - Under [Durable Execution And Control Store](20261019-durable_execution_and_control_store.md#physical-receipt-consumption), Core retains the exact physical receipt until the consuming Workspace outcome is durable and acknowledged; successor dispatch alone never retires it.
  - Across an outer connection loss, an authoritative successor MUST submit its single retained mutating result before issuing any effect poll; after NanoCore restart, result-only expectations are re-derived solely from immutable existing cleanup rows (`bridge.close`, `sandbox.delete`), accepted `final_status` closeout lineage, or environment-preparation candidate lineage without replaying commands.
  - For environment-preparation `image.acquire` and `image.build` ([Persistent Worker Volumes](20260910-persistent_worker_volumes.md)), NanoCore writes durable image settlement before acknowledging; a known identical settlement acknowledges a retained duplicate after restart even if the source candidate is dead and does not trigger poll-first unknown fencing. Only a validated local persistence failure on those two operations returns empty HTTP `503` as delivery deferral, causing NanoHost to retain the identical `requestId` and result bytes, wait the current default of `1 s` (`apps/nanohost/src/nanocore_session.rs`), and retry on the same connection before any lifecycle poll without retry-count or elapsed-time Epoch termination.
  - An unpending `sandbox.delete` result receives a delivery-discard empty `204` ([Retained Delete Result Discard](../decisions/20261003-retained_delete_result_discard.md)) only on the current authoritative connection when its `requestId` deterministically matches one unambiguous backend session row in `physical-cleaned` or `cleaned` state and its body is `{sandboxId, state: "deleted"}` for that Sandbox (pending expectations take precedence, and discard performs no mutation or capacity release).
- **Operation-specific contracts**:
  - **`bridge.open`**: Command carries the static non-secret `sandboxIntegrationBindingRef`, the exact existing `sandboxBindingRef` of the admitted Sandbox, and bounded declared Harness descriptors (never Turn, AEP, Context Package, route, Provider, or Vault credentials, bulk bytes, or host-exec selectors); after complete local acceptance, NanoHost retains only request identity, live Harness monitor, live bridge with its original bindings, and the settled redacted result. Both peers must support the revised closed command before enabling it.
  - **`image.inspect`, `storage.inspect`, and `storage.purge`**: Read-only `image.inspect` (returning verified local digest mount-layout, numeric ownership, `WorkingDir`, platform, and `Config.Env` facts without acquiring or building) and `storage.inspect` (reading exact `storageRef` and expected generation to distinguish initialized, missing, conflicted, unreadable, or attached state) use current request/result correlation only, with no cross-connection retention or Epoch invalidation on loss. `storage.purge` binds exact `storageRef`, expected generation, and current Core deletion authorization (never a caller host path), proves local identity and writer fencing before removing only that association, and if lost or partial remains `unknown` and fences that `storageRef` against attachment without invalidating unrelated healthy Epoch work.
  - **`image.build/input`**: After accepting `image.build` metadata, NanoHost calls `POST /api/nanohost/transport/effects/image.build/input` with `{}` and `x-openkit-request-id`; NanoCore returns `200 application/octet-stream` with single-valued `content-length`, `x-openkit-request-id`, `x-openkit-sha256` (`sha256:<64hex>`), and `x-openkit-byte-length` (`1..268,435,456`, matching `content-length` and metadata). NanoHost streams at most `65,536`-byte chunks on the single file-data reservation and verifies complete UTF-8 and digest before creating a build root, writing a Dockerfile, opening egress, or invoking Buildx.
  - **`reference.import`, `file.export`, and `workspace.collect`**: Per-AgentSession `reference.import` orders AEP first, then declared worker-supply files, then Context Package files into disjoint Session-private roots (initial AEP and static supply before `session.open`; Context before `turn.start`; never Sandbox-wide roots), staging and verifying canonical headers (`docs/specs/20260801-nanohost_workspace_data_boundary.md`) before running the fixed Sandbox helper. `file.export` binds `requestId`, Sandbox, AgentSession, Turn, slot, normalized relative path, maximum (`<= 256 MiB` for terminal transcript/evidence after accepted `final_status`, or remaining Worker Artifact capacity plus `1` sentinel byte for active Artifact submissions; ordinary file/import/Artifact ceilings admit up to `1,000,000,000` bytes plus `1` sentinel byte in `apps/nanohost/src/file_transfer.rs`), closed purpose, and `presence` (`required` or `optional`), using secure absence only below a proved slot root; NanoCore returns `204` only after verified fsynced atomic staging (or exact optional absence / active-submission refusal), and commits an Artifact ID only after its semantic tuple and receipt are durable. `workspace.collect` uses the same file-data reservation under `docs/specs/20260801-nanohost_workspace_data_boundary.md`.

### Private Harness Control Carriage And Session Environment

- **Seven fixed Harness operations over `/worker-control/harness/poll` and `result`**: The closed seven-operation Harness vocabulary is defined by `HarnessOperationSchema` in `packages/worker-protocol/src/harness-control.ts` (see [Protocol Identifiers And Enums](20261021-protocol_identifiers_and_enums.md#canonical-enums)), with one compare-and-swap interrupt delivery owner and shared supervisor (`packages/worker-shim/src/harness.ts`). Integration pulls commands and posts results exclusively over the current nested HTTP/2 connection via `POST /worker-control/harness/poll` and `POST /worker-control/harness/result` (`<= 64 KiB` control ceiling, at most one active request at a time, streamed directly without NanoHost buffering or command retention). NanoHost rejects any client-supplied `authorization`, `x-openkit-integration-binding`, or `x-openkit-harness-binding` header and injects the bridge's opaque non-secret `sandboxIntegrationBindingRef` (fixed per Integration lifetime and accepted by NanoCore only when matched to the current authoritative outer connection, Sandbox, Integration record, and—for delivered commands—the admitted private `harnessInstanceId`; neither ID is a bearer token or exposed to native children, AEPs, Context Packages, product records, or diagnostics). NanoHost also rejects a Sandbox-supplied `x-openkit-sandbox-binding` and stamps that header on the authenticated outer poll from the original bridge admission. The binding stays on that private hop. A legacy bridge or a missing or ambiguous Sandbox binding keeps the poll held. Core derives the release refusal execution ID from the authenticated target, physical Epoch and original Sandbox binding before reading a resident. A first refusal and compatible command selection and handoff require both original bindings to match. A bound release refusal returns 409 only after the exact Core order and complete Native drain. This adds no Host effect, fourth namespace, reverse request direction, second connection, or `bridge.open` credential.
- **Session-static `nativeEnvironment` ([Native Environment Managed Outside The Sandbox](../decisions/20260930-native_environment_managed_outside_the_sandbox.md))**: Public `session.open.nativeEnvironment` is delivered separately from Vault `runtimeEnvironment`, validated against AEP names and manifest size bounds within the control ceiling, and applied to the native child launched under `env -i` after fixed trusted bootstrap/interpreter and control trust/proxy bindings (never recovering image defaults from inherited Harness env or mutating a resident process in place). Each Turn rechecks the session-static public projection and credential-name equality against its AEP, and lost native-start or close acknowledgement remains `unknown` and fenced.
- **Startup diagnostics and Worker markers**: A `turn.start` dependency refusal may carry the closed startup failure explanation owned by [Worker Turn Reliability Envelope](20260531-worker_turn_reliability_envelope.md#durable-git-fetch-failure-explanation) under exact operation, sequence, identical-result redelivery, and durable result-before-acknowledgement rules (mismatched outer stage/reason or malformed explanation is rejected by Core before settlement, adding no spool, replay, or extra wait, with absent observation treated as unknown cause). Closed Worker marker variants (`docs/specs/20260731-operational_telemetry_standardization.md`) ride existing worker control and are retained by the execution journal (`docs/specs/20261019-durable_execution_and_control_store.md#unverified-worker-markers`) as unverified point events, never control decisions, SDKs, OTLP exporters, or new egress.

## Liveness And Timers

Route separation on a shared HTTP/2 connection MUST NOT depend on deprecated RFC 9113 HTTP/2 stream priority; worker-control traffic (`turn.interrupt`, heartbeats, sequence, and terminal evidence) stays live under saturating inference or capability load through explicit stream reservations, receive windows, and chunking (`apps/nanohost/src/sandbox_bridge.rs`, `packages/worker-shim/src/integration-client.ts`, `packages/worker-shim/src/lifecycle-deadline.ts`), and backpressure in one family MUST NOT authorize dropping, reordering, replaying, or cross-family reinterpretation in another.

### Stream, Window, And Liveness Envelope

Chosen numeric inputs below are calibratable defaults ([Capability Stream Ceiling Default](../decisions/20261003-capability_stream_ceiling_default.md), [Shared Capability Capacity Acceptance](../decisions/20261003-shared_capability_capacity_acceptance.md)), while derivation formulas, positive unclaimed connection headroom, and the independently reserved worker-control floor remain binding (whole-MiB window sizing is convenience only, and changing any input recomputes all derived sums in the same amendment):

| Bound | Current default or derived bound | Scope / derivation and admissibility rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connection-level receive window | `5 MiB` (current default) | Both nested and outer HTTP/2 sessions. |
| Nested session concurrent streams | `16` (derived sum) | Sandbox Integration to NanoHost: `4` reserved `/worker-control/*` (never reallocated), `8` `/inference/*` (current default), `4` `/capabilities/*` (current default; `0` when non-callable). |
| Outer session concurrent streams | `18` (derived sum) | NanoHost to NanoCore: `1` reserved control/readiness, `1` reserved fixed file-data (`<= 1` active across file effects, `workspace.collect` result, and `image.build/input`), `4` reserved `/worker-control/*`, `8` `/inference/*`, `4` `/capabilities/*` (`0` when non-callable). |
| Per-stream initial receive window | `256 KiB` (current default) | Both sessions; fixed file-data stream releases capacity as the application consumes at most `64 KiB` at a time. |
| Per-family in-flight `DATA` ceilings | Derived: `stream ceiling * 256 KiB` | `/worker-control/*`: `1 MiB` (also the reserved connection-window floor); `/inference/*`: `2 MiB`; `/capabilities/*`: `1 MiB`; outer control/readiness `256 KiB` + file-data `256 KiB` (`512 KiB` Host `DATA` aggregate in `apps/nanohost/src/sandbox_bridge.rs`). Nested family sum is `4 MiB` (`1 MiB` headroom inside `5 MiB`); outer family sum is `4.5 MiB` (`512 KiB` headroom inside `5 MiB`). |
| Max forwarded `DATA` per inference write | `64 KiB` (current default) | Both sessions. |
| Interrupt delivery `p99` under saturation | `<= 2,000 ms` (current default target) | Admissibility ceiling `<= 5,000 ms`, measured from committed product authorization and private Harness `turn.interrupt` enqueue to shared-supervisor observation. Poll cadence belongs to `docs/specs/20260703-worker_control_protocol.md`; raising this bound requires a matching cadence amendment there. |
| Heartbeat delivery delay under saturation | `<= 10 s` (current default bound `B`) | Under [Worker Runtime Communication Model](20260629-worker_runtime_communication_model.md), `margin = deadline - interval - B` MUST remain `>= 1` full heartbeat interval (`I`). At current defaults (`deadline = 30 s`, `I = 1 s`, `B = 10 s`), `margin = 19 s` and the accepted heartbeat gap bound is `I + B = 11 s` (admissibility limit `B <= 28 s` at those defaults). Any missed heartbeat deadline is a mechanism failure. |
| Bridge re-establishment | `<= 30 s` target, `<= 120 s` hard bound (current defaults) | Measured from NanoHost route-loss observation (including detection and predecessor-closure proof) to successor route-ready. Hard bound MUST leave at least half of the worker-control outage budget unconsumed from the same route-loss instant (`300 s - 120 s = 180 s >= 150 s`); exceeding it stops silent retries and reports route failure through attempt/worker-control/reliability owners. |

### Operation, Collection, Produced-Fact, And Request Timers

- **Private Harness enqueue budget**: The private Harness-operation wait budget starts at Core enqueue (including commands never polled) and is never reset by acceptance or delivery (`apps/nanocore/src/runtime/nanohost-session-dispatch.ts`). Expiry removes the command from future delivery, rejects its waiter, and records the failed/unknown operation and cleanup disposition without asserting process absence, authorizing another writer, or fabricating Task completion.
- **Harness poll and result request outage allowance ([Harness Carriage Deadline](../decisions/20261020-harness_carriage_deadline.md))**: For private `/worker-control/harness/poll` and `/worker-control/harness/result` requests (`docs/specs/20260703-worker_control_protocol.md#harness-pull-and-result-envelopes`), response headers and complete bounded body collection share the remaining original request outage allowance on a single abort timer (`packages/worker-shim/src/harness.ts`, `packages/worker-shim/src/integration-client.ts`); there is no separate `1 s` header-wait cutoff.
- **`workspace.collect` result delivery timer ([Workspace Collection Resource Limits](../decisions/20260930-workspace_collection_resource_limits.md))**: A `workspace.collect` result delivery has one absolute `120 s` deadline covering stream admission, capacity waits, body delivery, and complete acknowledgement; expiry leaves the result `unknown`, closes the physical-session attempt through the reconnect owner, and clears collection result retention without reporting success or authorizing rescan or redelivery.
- **Epoch-local produced-fact buffer**: Per active Sandbox, buffered produced facts are bounded to at most `8 MiB` and a maximum age equal to the worker-control outage budget (`300 s` current default). Reaching either bound is a truthful failure through the owning record's contract—never a silent drop, summary, or reorder—and the buffer is disposable Epoch-local state that never survives its Epoch or serves as recovery, readiness, or capacity authority.

## Errors And Failure

### Authentication, Credential, And TLS Trust Failures

- **Uniform non-echoing authentication failure**: Unknown, malformed, wrong-type, wrong-scope, wrong-NanoHost, wrong-deployment, expired, rotated, revoked, or stale-generation tokens produce the same non-echoing authentication failure (`401`); no response, Audit record, log, metric, trace, or diagnostic may reveal the presented secret, its hash, or which verification step failed.
- **No slot fallback or probing**: Because authentication failures are indistinguishable, a rejected presented credential NEVER falls back to the other slot or infers a pending rotation cutover; it keeps NanoHost non-ready until an explicit `server-admin` issuance or rotation action. When both slots are missing, unreadable, wrong-owner, wrong-mode, ambiguous, or unproved, NanoHost stays non-ready without looping.
- **Failed enrollment sink compensation**: If NanoCore cannot prove the enrollment sink write or if the identity/Token transaction fails, it leaves the Token nonexistent/unusable and the NanoHost identity absent or decommissioned, cleaning up only files still proved to belong to that attempt's exact Token ID (never deleting missing, malformed, replaced, or foreign files). If exact absence after cleanup cannot be proved, that slot blocks later enrollment until explicit operator proof of emptiness; recovery requires a new explicit `server-admin` action with no implicit retry, rebootstrap, printed recovery secret, identity-row replacement, or old-Token revival.
- **Overlap expiry and revocation behavior**: When a rotation overlap deadline or credential expiry arrives without a winning successor connection, verification fails closed, NanoCore fences the predecessor session, and NanoHost becomes non-ready for new work with no extended grace, reactivation, or auto-bootstrap. On revocation or credential loss, already-running local AgentSessions may continue only under existing bounded disconnect/attempt rules while the local NanoHost remains healthy and the revoked credential cannot reauthorize or open a new connection (granting no extended attempt time, route authority, output acceptance, inferred completion, or automatic retry).
- **TLS verification failures**: Missing, invalid, expired, hostname-mismatched, or untrusted server certificates (including a CA certificate presented as the server leaf) prevent an authoritative session and keep NanoHost non-ready within the startup deadline. A configured CA is exclusive and never falls back to platform roots, trust-on-first-use, verification bypass, plaintext downgrade, SSH tunnel, alternate endpoints, or bearer-only acceptance. An initial definite TLS rejection reports a closed TLS verification cause (without raw certificate errors, endpoints, trust paths, or secrets) and ends that startup attempt rather than entering physical-close retry or waiting for a startup timeout; successor failures follow healthy-Epoch reconnect rules.

### Predecessor Fencing, Reconnect, And Poll-First Fail-Stop

- **Strictly increasing generations and predecessor fencing**: Every new physical connection receives a strictly increasing `connection_generation` (never reused). NanoCore MUST reject or fence the predecessor connection before the successor may report readiness, and never allows two authoritative sessions; if predecessor fencing cannot be proved, the successor remains non-authoritative and carries no work. Any late frame, replay, heartbeat, result, or readiness report from a fenced predecessor is rejected without mutating successor state. Server-observed physical close, transport failure, or authentication loss immediately persists that exact generation as fenced and non-ready while retaining it as the durable high-water mark.
- **Readiness and reconnect boundaries**: Every successor physical connection MUST complete its own admission and `POST /api/nanohost/transport/session/readiness` handshake before effect polling; a healthy reconnect re-reports the same live fresh-empty coordinator witness without rebuilding the Epoch or relaunching work, but never clears a prior cleanup fence. A malformed, synthetic, stale, future, candidate, fenced, wrong-connection, or non-`204` readiness response is terminal for that physical attempt (whereas cancellation or loss from an observed physical close uses the `reconnect` disposition).
- **Fixed-effect first carriage**: NanoCore completes encoding, source preparation and owner reads before the first native HTTP/2 write. At that write it checks the exact queued operation, physical connection and RuntimeTarget, current route admission, and the initiating owner's original authority and remaining allowance. It latches acceptance and writes in one synchronous turn. An accepted build's one-shot input uses the same rule on the accepted connection. After possible carriage, only exact result settlement or the existing unknown and fence disposition is available.
- **Retained-result continuity vs poll-first `unknown`**: If an authoritative successor has no eligible retained mutating result and starts its first effect poll, NanoCore marks the prior unresolved accepted effect `unknown` once, preserves its cleanup owner and capacity fence, and rejects and fences that successor connection without redispatching the effect. From the start of that first poll until NanoHost completely receives the poll response, any cancellation, reset, timeout, or physical close is `terminal` and fail-stops the coordinator and complete Runtime Epoch (requiring a fresh coordinator after fail-stop to clear the fence).
- **Preparation `503` vs terminal `503`**: Only an empty HTTP `503` on validated local persistence failure for environment-preparation `image.acquire` or `image.build` defers result delivery (waiting for persistence recovery without interrupting resident Workers while blocking new lifecycle polls); a malformed/non-empty `503` or any `503` on another operation is terminal, and independent member, identity, containment, or connection faults keep their normal fences.

### Effect Result Failures, File/Build Refusals, And Closed Diagnostics

- **Definite `effect_failed` vs export absence/refusal**:
  - For an ordinary bounded-JSON effect result where the local owner proved a definite failure with no unclassified residual effect, the sole failure body is exact `{"requestId":"<requestId>","failureCode":"effect_failed"}` (carrying no extra fields, paths, outputs, or error text). NanoCore acknowledges it on the current authoritative connection with empty `204`, settles the pending effect once as failed, and retains bounded completed-result identity for duplicate recognition while continuing the session (`effect_failed` never applies to physical/result-delivery uncertainty, partial `storage.purge`, uncertain create/delete/`bridge.open`, present-file `file.export`, or `workspace.collect`).
  - On `file.export`, an optional absent file returns exact `{"requestId":"<requestId>","state":"absent"}` (`presence: "optional"` only), and a proved active-submission non-regular or multi-link file refusal returns exact `{"requestId":"<requestId>","state":"refused","reasonCode":"artifact_file_not_regular"}` after secure root/path checks and clean helper completion (`exit 0` present file vs `exit 2` empty-output optional absence), which NanoCore acknowledges once with `204` without publication and without deleting a healthy Sandbox.
  - On `reference.import`, a pre-effect metadata or verified-body refusal with a valid `64`-hex `requestId` may report definite `effect_failed` with a fixed local diagnostic cause and continue polling, whereas a missing/invalid `requestId` is terminal and any physical stream, Sandbox import, or result-delivery uncertainty retains existing cleanup/Epoch fences.
- **`image.build/input` and outer file-data failures**: Malformed, mismatched, non-UTF-8, or forbidden `image.build/input` requests/responses fail closed before `BuildPlan`. Candidate, fenced, stale, wrong-operation, unaccepted, mismatched, or repeated same-generation fetches return `409` with no bytes; over-ceiling bodies return `413`; private source/stream failures return redacted `500` (`docs/specs/20260801-nanohost_workspace_data_boundary.md` governs `400`/`413`/`409`/redacted `500` on outer file-data streams with no JSON fallback). Reset, cancellation, timeout, or physical close before complete Dockerfile verification removes partial bytes and records definite `effect_failed` without starting a build root or backend effect, and the Dockerfile is never refetched, resumed, or replayed; after complete verification and build admission, connection loss never restarts the build, which finishes once and submits only its unchanged result on a successor.
- **No command replay across reconnects**: Transport reconnect never replays an accepted NanoHost effect command: a live NanoHost finishes an accepted local create, delete, build, export, or `bridge.open` to one definite result and submits only that exact complete result under the same re-derived `requestId` on an authoritative successor (for accepted `bridge.open`, preserving the live Harness monitor and bridge with zero repeated Start/create/launch/open; for unaccepted `bridge.open` or uncertain import/export, never re-polling or inferring launch and widening cleanup through exact Sandbox deletion or Epoch invalidation when deletion is uncertain; for Turn export, accepted `final_status`—never process death—remains the per-Turn export barrier while workspace collection gates the next Turn; image/slot failures alone do not invalidate a healthy Epoch, whereas Gateway, backend, or containment uncertainty invalidates the complete Epoch). Core rejects changed or ambiguous duplicates, re-derives durable lineage, and requires a new owner-authorized request for any later logical retry.
- **Private Harness reset recovery and `1,250 ms` non-receipt proof**: Across a nested bridge interruption or peer stream reset, Integration retries only the same immutable request within the remaining route-outage budget, and a successor bridge may redeliver an exact result or prove command non-receipt via its live per-Harness `nextSequence` only for the same `sandboxIntegrationBindingRef`, `harnessInstanceId`, operation ID, and sequence (reusing original Core in-memory wire bytes without minting new tokens or overlapping polling with execution). Every peer poll reset is observed regardless of local write completion: absent matching progress in the carrying poll causes immediate `unknown`, and an advertised proof path has at most `1,250 ms` (`apps/nanocore/src/runtime/nanohost-session-dispatch.ts`) to supply non-receipt proof before becoming `unknown`, stopping admission, and invoking the cleanup fence. Bridge re-establishment and Gateway/bridge reconnects require current Epoch/Gateway/Sandbox/Supervisor/Harness identity and predecessor closure proof without skipping proof, replaying requests, adopting unproved pairs, or extending the worker outage budget.
- **Closed outer-session, HTTP/2, and TLS failure classifications**:
  - Outer-session failure classification (`OuterSessionDisposition`, `OuterSessionStage`, and `OuterSessionOperation` in `apps/nanohost/src/nanocore_session.rs` and `apps/nanohost/src/main.rs`; see [Protocol Identifiers And Enums](20261021-protocol_identifiers_and_enums.md#canonical-enums)) classifies each failure by closed disposition (reconnect vs terminal), six stage variants, the `12` fixed effect operations on poll/execute/result stages (or `none` otherwise), and received HTTP status `100..599` (or `none`), printing `nanohost outer session failure: disposition=<disposition> stage=<stage> operation=<operation> status=<status>` once on terminal nonzero exit with no IDs, paths, endpoints, headers, bodies, raw errors, or credentials.
  - Physical HTTP/2 driver completion (`format_h2_driver_close` in `apps/nanohost/src/nanocore_session.rs`) logs a value-free observation from closed HTTP/2 reason (`unknown` fallback), I/O error kind (`other` fallback), initiator, and driver task completion (keeping intentional caller abort distinct from peer failure) plus the retained definite result's operation name or `none`. Before HTTP/2 erases a typed `rustls` error, the TLS adapter (`format_tls_processing_failure` in `apps/nanohost/src/nanocore_session.rs`) emits `nanohost TLS processing failed: cause=<class>` using the closed eight-class `rustls::Error` mapping (`other` fallback) without raw certificate, alert, or peer strings.

## Compatibility

- **Shared transport revision (`x-openkit-nanohost-transport-revision`)**: Native admission sends the single shared revision from `apps/nanocore/src/auth/nanohost-transport-revision.json` (currently literal `2`, embedded in `apps/nanohost/src/nanocore_session.rs`), and a successful response echoes that exact header. After Token and native physical-connection validation and before generation allocation, NanoCore rejects a missing or unequal value with HTTP `409` and `nanohost_protocol_mismatch`, and NanoHost treats that response or a missing/unequal echo as terminal admission failure before readiness or effect polling. The revision is bumped only when native framing or command contracts change and adds no version negotiation, compatibility range, migration, or retained state.
- **Sole authentication and reconnect semantics**: Dedicated bearer NanoHost Token authentication over server-authenticated TLS (or exact same-host loopback HTTP/2) is the sole transport authentication path, with no client certificates, mTLS, `server-admin` token reuse, or alternate authentication path. Transport reconnect restores carriage only after existing owners prove exact identity and lineage, without creating a new NanoHost identity, Runtime Epoch, AgentSession, attempt, Sandbox, Worker process, or Turn.
- **Cross-language protocol ownership**: `@openkit/protocol` and `@openkit/worker-protocol` own cross-language OpenKit records; `apps/nanohost` consumes only generated language-neutral schemas, fixtures, Rust projections, and bounded transport parsers without creating a second authoritative Rust schema family, importing NanoCore modules, adding an application Web framework, or adding a durable product store.
- **Pull-only nested HTTP/2 roles and measurement-gated fallback**:
  - Under `docs/specs/20260703-worker_control_protocol.md`, worker-control command delivery is pull-only, so Sandbox Integration is the nested HTTP/2 client, NanoHost is the server, and no inbound request path into the Sandbox is granted. Adding backend command push, reversing HTTP/2 roles, or exposing an inbound Sandbox listener is outside this contract and requires joint acceptance by this specification and the worker-control owner before implementation (`#395`).
  - Absent recorded real-stock saturation qualification evidence that one byte stream per active Sandbox cannot meet a named worker-control liveness bound, exactly one byte stream per active Sandbox is the contract; a second dedicated `/worker-control/*` byte stream (with its own credential and predecessor fence, carrying no inference or capability traffic) is permitted only when authorized by that recorded measurement failure and is never a configuration or performance option.
  - `AgentSession` identity, retained native proof, lifecycle, terminality, and no-relist supply rules remain owned by `docs/specs/20260704-agent_session_continuity.md` and the four Worker adapters—[Codex](20260716-codex_worker_adapter.md), [OpenCode](20260716-opencode_worker_adapter.md), [Pi](20260716-pi_worker_adapter.md), and [DeepSeek](20260930-deepseek_worker_adapter.md)—which bind their Session-local loopback bearer carriers (`127.0.0.1:17892`) without re-listing supply in place or gaining Host/Gateway authentication.

## Acceptance

- End-to-end transport and reconnect verification across physical connection loss and NanoCore restart proves native server-context admission (`{}`), transactional generation progression (`1` or high-water plus `1`) and predecessor fencing, fixed-path readiness (`204`) before polling, the 12 fixed effect pairs and single-stream `image.build/input` / `reference.import` / `file.export` / `workspace.collect` file-data carriage (including `final_status` vs active-Turn export barriers and next-Turn collection gating under `docs/specs/20260801-nanohost_workspace_data_boundary.md`), retained-result-before-poll settlement, empty-`503` preparation-image deferral vs `204` cleaned-delete discard, definite `effect_failed` vs terminal successor first-poll fail-stop, and value-free closed outer-session, HTTP/2, and TLS diagnostics.
- Credential, trust, and route-isolation verification proves `okt_` CSPRNG entropy and constant-time hash-only verification, non-overwrite enrollment and decommissioned-identity reactivation, two-slot (`A`/`B`) companion-generation selection without mtime/filename/length inference or rejection fallback, non-disruptive overlap rotation and abort without Epoch invalidation, immediate revocation fencing, explicit decommission cleanup of both slots/store/evidence, non-loopback TLS verification against platform or exclusive configured CA roots, redacted Audit emission, and independent 32-byte per-Turn route-family tokens (`sha256` attempt projections) across NanoCore restarts.
- Real-stock saturating-load qualification (`>= 60 s` continuous inference stream and `DATA` ceiling load under `docs/specs/20260802-nanohost_runtime_and_transport.md`) proves that reserved worker-control streams, explicit window releases, and `64 KiB` inference chunking satisfy the `<= 2,000 ms` (`<= 5,000 ms` admissibility) interrupt `p99` bound and `<= 10 s` heartbeat delay bound (`margin = 19 s >= 1 s`), peak intermediate buffering across the nested flow-control layers remains bounded and observed, bridge re-establishment completes within the `120 s` hard bound leaving at least half of the `300 s` worker outage budget unconsumed, NanoHost-initiated invalidations export the bounded redacted Epoch Invalidation Report before fencing while absent-Host invalidations record a recovery disposition note (`docs/specs/20260802-nanohost_runtime_and_transport.md`) with neither read by recovery/readiness/capacity decisions, observed Epoch rebuild time and uncertain-outcome rate are durably recorded against the shared-Epoch falsifiable premise (`docs/specs/20260802-nanohost_runtime_and_transport.md`, `docs/specs/20260703-audit_usage_evidence_records.md`), and a NanoCore outage leaves the Worker's process, attempt, and sequence intact with inference stalled/unavailable (never locally satisfied or claiming progress in diagnostics) while the `8 MiB` / `300 s` produced-fact buffer and no-authority/no-response-cache rules hold.
