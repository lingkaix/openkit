# Goal

## Owns

- The Goal, its work-intent cards, its immutable Plan versions, the wake marker, the completion disposition, and the Goal operations.
- The product contract of the Coordinator: what that role may decide inside an approved Plan version, and what it must not decide.
- The one-way removal of existing Goal-owned data, and which non-Goal records stay usable when that data is gone.

## Does Not Own

- Task execution, Task Threads, and worker Turns. Task Mode owns a Task as its Thread and Turns.
- Pending Request records, resolution, and delivery. Pending Requests owns Plan approval and completion acceptance as command-intent approvals.
- The internal agent loop, its tools mechanism, and its fuses. The internal agent runtime owns that loop. The Coordinator is a role over it.
- The scheduler, Sandbox selection, and worker admission. No Goal pin exists.
- Knowledge pages, proposals, and retrieval. Conventions are Knowledge when a person publishes them there.
- The worker Context Package. A Goal-admitted Task uses the ordinary Task package.
- Audit events and command receipts. They stay readable and are not Goal progression authority.
- The operation-definition architecture, the operation catalog, transport, and wire names. Goal owns semantic operation behavior, not the operation-definition architecture; its surfaces derive from the accepted single definition under [Operation Definition](20261002-operation_definition.md).
- Chat replies, Action Center presentation, Artifact Review, and Workspace Sync Review.

## Summary

A Goal is one continuously pursued outcome in one Workspace, plus the authority a person has delegated to pursue it. The person keeps the intent and the work-intent cards and approves one exact Plan version. The responsible person, or an administrator under the administrator authority ruling, accepts the exact candidate through Pending Requests; the request and the Goal disposition identify the actual deciding actor and never attribute an administrator's decision to the affected person. The Coordinator never approves its own Plan or completion. [Pending Requests](20260930-pending_requests.md) states the landed resolver rule for that administrator decision. The Coordinator pursues the approved version by admitting ordinary Tasks. Goal Mode is this composition of existing NanoCore capabilities. It is not a separate runtime, workflow engine, graph or recipe contract, or record family for execution status. [Goal Redesign Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_redesign_rulings.md) records why.

## Decision

The rules below are the contract. The rulings record the reasons and the rejected alternatives.

A Goal stays one process while the person revises the outcome, the scope, and the cards. Planning is that process. It is not a separate workflow or a planning session. A material change of commitment does not by itself create a new Goal. A new Goal is a deliberate split or an independently managed outcome. [Continuous Goal Intent And Plan Approval](../decisions/20260924-continuous_goal_intent_and_plan_approval.md) is the reason for this continuity.

Every new Plan version requires approval of that exact version. A refinement wholly inside the approved version is an execution choice and needs no new approval. An unchanged field, or the size of a diff, does not prove that a change is immaterial. The person's instruction authorizes the intent change they asked for. Derived method, cost, permission, and external effect stay with their own owners.

A failed proposal leaves the Goal open. It does not fail the Goal, and it does not clear an active Plan version.

Inside an approved Plan version, the Coordinator may admit and adjust bounded Tasks without a per-Task confirmation. A new material commitment needs a new exact approval. Effect-specific approvals stay with their effect owners.

The responsible person, or an administrator under the administrator authority ruling, accepts the exact candidate through Pending Requests; the request and the Goal disposition identify the actual deciding actor and never attribute an administrator's decision to the affected person. The Coordinator never approves its own Plan or completion. The Coordinator, an evaluation, and a reviewer may recommend. A worker completion or a review verdict does not complete the Goal. [Pending Requests](20260930-pending_requests.md) states the landed resolver rule for that administrator decision.

Existing Goal-owned data is deleted with the new implementation. It is not migrated and not kept as read-only Goal history. Records outside Goal ownership that mention a Goal stay usable.

The acting identity is an authorization input on every operation. This specification creates no agent-identity record and grants no Policy Kernel authority. [Interface Unification Rulings](../decisions/20261002-interface_unification_rulings.md) reserves that later work and states the board rule: the person and the Coordinator use the same card operation, and the person does not edit observed execution status.

Plan eligibility after a card edit, the completion candidate, and the closing of open Plan and completion requests are rules in the sections below. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

## Goal

### Definition And Exclusions

A Goal is one continuous desired outcome in one Workspace. It has current human intent, one responsible person, the Thread that addresses the Coordinator, a pointer to at most one proposed Plan version, a pointer to at most one active Plan version, a terminal disposition when it has ended, and the wake marker.

It is not a running agent, a workflow instance, a status the person edits, or a roll-up that succeeds because every card looks done. It has no autonomy class, budget, pause, or resume.

### Durable Authority And Projection

The Goal record is Core authority for that outcome and for the pointers, disposition, and wake marker. The board, summaries, and Items are projections. The Coordinator Thread is an ordinary Thread. A protocol `plan` Item, when one is emitted, is a visible snapshot and is not the approved bytes.

### Lifecycle

Create records the intent and the Coordinator Thread. It authorizes no worker. Revising intent keeps the same Goal and appends intent history. The approved Plan bytes do not change. An intent revision does not by itself invalidate the active Plan version. An accepted completion or an explicit cancel ends new admissions and keeps the reason. A failed Coordinator Turn leaves the Goal open. Retry is a new Turn or a new operation from current records. Recovery rereads the Goal, its Plan versions, its cards, and the linked Threads. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

### Conflict, Missing, Stale, Restart, And Dependency Failure

A stale intent or card revision conflicts and writes nothing. A missing linked Task is unresolved evidence. It is not permission to invent a result or to start a duplicate. Restart reconstructs from the Goal, its versions, its cards, and the linked Threads. A failed worker, a missing external result, or an unknown effect does not select the disposition. The Goal stays open until the person accepts completion or cancels it.

### Acceptance

A person can create a Goal and revise its intent. An approved Plan version is distinguishable from current intent after a later edit. A worker completion leaves the Goal open.

## Work-Intent Card

### Definition And Exclusions

A work-intent card is one stable desired contribution inside one Goal. It has a description, a relative priority, a cancellation, an expected revision, and links to zero or more ordinary Task Threads reserved to pursue it. Each link names the Plan version and card revision cited at reservation; the link alone does not prove Task Turn admission.

A card is not a worker, a Turn, an admitted Task input, or a user-written execution status. It has no dependency executor. A dependency exists only when the card's own text states one.

### Durable Authority And Projection

The card is Goal-owned authority for that contribution. The board joins the card to Task, Turn, and Pending Request state and stores none of that state again. Admission and its time come from the linked Task Thread's first admitted Turn, never from the reservation citation. A reservation with no Turn remains unadmitted, including after a definite pre-effect refusal.

### Lifecycle

Create records the contribution and starts no Task. Edit writes a new revision and leaves every already admitted Task input unchanged. Cancel records cancellation, refuses new admissions for that card, and asks the Task owner to interrupt a linked running Task. Completed attempts stay. Several Tasks may pursue one card. Retry of a write uses the current revision.

### Conflict, Missing, Stale, Restart, And Dependency Failure

A write names the revision it read. A mismatch conflicts and writes nothing. A missing Task link is not "not started" and is not permission to start a duplicate. A cancellation that races a dispatch is settled by admitting only against the current card and the current Plan version. Restart rereads the card and the linked Threads.

### Acceptance

A card can exist with no Task. The same card can show more than one retained attempt. The person and the Coordinator issue the same create, edit, and cancel operations. The Coordinator learns of an edit by reading on its next admitted Turn. Dragging a card, or marking it done, does not complete the Goal and does not write execution status.

## Plan Version

### Definition And Exclusions

A Plan version is an immutable version of what the person authorized the Coordinator to pursue. Its commitment is the intent basis, the card revisions it authorizes, the adjustments the Coordinator may make inside that commitment, and the evidence a completion candidate must address.

It is not a second objective, a live copy of work, a worker status, a shell script, or a Sandbox procedure. It has no token budget, autonomy class, reviewer enum, task disposition list, or dependency graph.

### Durable Authority And Projection

The version bytes and their digest are the authority. The Goal points at the proposed version and the active version. Later intent or card edits do not change those bytes. The digest identifies the exact commitment. Approval compares it to the bytes being approved. The digest algorithm is fixed with the persisted record format. It is not a product rule here.

### Lifecycle

Propose creates a version and raises that version's approval. It does not activate the version. Approval selects that exact version and no other when the Goal owner consumes the grant, and it does not start worker work by itself. Rejection leaves the Goal and any still-valid active version in place. A failed proposal does the same. Supersession is a later version, not an edit. A new proposal invalidates any older open Plan proposal of the same Goal at once. Open means the request is still pending. The person sees the closing reason where that request is shown. The active version is not edited and is not invalidated by that proposal. [Pending Requests](20260930-pending_requests.md) owns the request ending. A refinement inside the approved version is recorded as an execution choice and creates no version. Changes to scope, deliverables, non-goals, constraints, effect or permission boundaries, cost or time commitments, acceptance criteria, output audience, important architecture or method, risk posture, or verification requirements are material; uncertainty about whether a change remains authorized requires a new exact approval rather than classification as an execution refinement. Ordinary methods and adjustments already permitted by the approved version remain execution choices. Whether the Coordinator may admit work for a card whose current revision differs from the revision the active Plan version recorded is decided by that version's permitted adjustments. The Coordinator judges whether the current card revision is within those adjustments. When it is not, the Coordinator proposes a new Plan version instead of admitting that work. The admission still cites the card revision and the Plan version. An intent revision does not by itself invalidate the active version. A new material commitment creates a new version and a new approval. Retry after failure is a new proposal from current records. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

### Conflict, Missing, Stale, Restart, And Dependency Failure

Approval and grant consumption recheck that the version bytes and digest are unchanged, that the actor is currently authorized, that the Goal is open, and the Pending Request conditions: approval needs a pending request, and consumption needs a granted, unconsumed and non-invalidated grant. A later intent revision or card edit does not by itself make an unchanged proposed version ineligible, end its pending request, or invalidate its unconsumed grant; the version's recorded intent basis and card revisions identify the exact commitment and are not equality fences against the current intent or cards. A newer proposal ends only older pending Plan requests and does not invalidate an already granted approval. The proposed-version eligibility decision is recorded in [a decision record](../decisions/20261003-proposed_plan_eligibility_after_edits.md). Activation changes neither the current intent nor the cards and authorizes no worker work by itself; every later admission checks the current intent and cancellation, and the Coordinator judges the current card against the active version's permitted adjustments. An intent revision does not by itself make the active version ineligible. A current card revision is eligible for admission when the Coordinator judges it to be within the active version's permitted adjustments, and the Coordinator proposes a new Plan version instead of admitting when it judges that the revision is not. An obsolete proposal does not replace a later active version. A note at approval time that changes the commitment is a new version, not a grant of the old one. Restart reads the version bytes and the Goal pointers. It does not infer a version from an Item or from model memory. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

### Acceptance

The approved bytes stay identical after a card edit. Approving one version never executes an unreviewed later version. An admission cites the current card revision and the active Plan version. A card revision the Coordinator judges to be outside that version's permitted adjustments is not admitted on that version. A failed proposal leaves the Goal open. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

## Wake Marker

### Definition And Exclusions

The wake marker is two fields on the Goal. One is the change revision. The other is the revision the last Coordinator Turn considered. The marker means that another read is required. It is not an inbox, a bus, an event payload, a due time, or a Coordinator-run record.

### Durable Authority And Projection

The source records remain the authority. The marker is bookkeeping so a later Turn notices that those records changed. No projection may treat the marker as a copy of the event.

### Lifecycle

The owner that commits a Goal-scoped fact advances the change revision in the same commit as that fact. This is an acceptance predicate. An admitted Coordinator Turn records only the revision it read. A change during that Turn remains for the next admission. A failed Turn does not spin. A newer revision, or an explicit retry, admits another Turn. There is no timer, due check, or repeated-work breaker.

### Conflict, Missing, Stale, Restart, And Dependency Failure

A missing marker is not a damaged Goal. The next admission reads the source records. Contradictory source records fail through their own owners. Restart sees a change revision that is ahead of the considered revision and admits on that fact. A Task completion and a card edit remain visible after restart because their commits advanced the marker.

### Acceptance

After a restart, a Coordinator Turn still considers a Task that ended and a card the person edited. The Turn reads the source records. The commit that recorded the Task's terminal fact is the same commit that advanced the marker. If that shared commit cannot be shown, the implementation stops and adds the missing durable fact on the owner that actually commits the Task result.

Core admits the Coordinator Turn when the marker is ahead and the Goal's Thread can accept a Turn. The opportunities are the committing command when that Thread is idle, a terminal barrier of the Coordinator Thread or of a linked Task Thread, and boot. Those are the same three opportunities Pending Requests already uses for delivery. This specification does not redefine that mechanism. Before each effect, the operation rechecks current intent, cancellation, Plan version, and actor. A card edit does not steer a running worker. Interrupting a worker uses the Task owner's interrupt.

## Completion Disposition

### Definition And Exclusions

The completion disposition is the Goal's terminal record that one exact completion candidate was accepted, or that the person canceled the Goal. The candidate names the Goal intent, the Plan version, the evidence, and the unresolved work. The responsible person, or an administrator under the administrator authority ruling, accepts the exact candidate through Pending Requests; the request and the Goal disposition identify the actual deciding actor and never attribute an administrator's decision to the affected person. The Coordinator never approves its own Plan or completion. [Pending Requests](20260930-pending_requests.md) states the landed resolver rule for that administrator decision.

Worker completion, a review verdict, quiescence, elapsed time, and model confidence are not the disposition. An evaluation may recommend. Its absence does not block the acceptance decision. There is no verification record and no private completion tool.

### Durable Authority And Projection

The disposition on the Goal is the authority that the Goal has ended. The Pending Request is the authority for the grant or refusal. Items may show the decision. They do not replace either record.

### Lifecycle

The Coordinator calls accept completion with one candidate. The Coordinator has no authority to accept, so that call becomes a Pending Request whose exact intent is the captured call, in the same pattern as an approval-required tool call. No operation is added. The responsible person, or an administrator under the administrator authority ruling, accepts the exact candidate through Pending Requests; the request and the Goal disposition identify the actual deciding actor and never attribute an administrator's decision to the affected person. The Coordinator never approves its own Plan or completion. The grant does not by itself execute the call. The Coordinator's Turn remains the delivery opportunity for that granted intent, and delivery is not itself proof of consumption. The Goal owner consumes that completion grant through the same synchronous re-evaluation-and-claim boundary after its own checks; invalidation that wins before the claim prevents the effect, and that consumption executes the captured call, records the granting actor as the decider, writes the disposition, and ends new admissions. Rejection by that same deciding actor leaves the Goal open. Cancel is a separate operation. It writes its own disposition and ends new admissions. It also ends every open Plan approval and completion request of that Goal at once as invalidated, with the reason that the Goal was cancelled. Open means the request is still pending. A granted but unconsumed grant of the cancelled Goal is refused with a conflict when its consumption is attempted, and that attempt neither activates a Plan version nor writes a Goal completion disposition; Pending Requests owns the recorded non-execution. The person sees the closing reason where the request is shown. [Pending Requests](20260930-pending_requests.md) owns the request ending. Retry after rejection is a new candidate from current records. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

### Conflict, Missing, Stale, Restart, And Dependency Failure

Acceptance rechecks that the candidate still matches current intent, the named Plan version, and the named evidence. A stale candidate is refused and does not end the Goal. A granted but unconsumed completion grant of a cancelled Goal is refused with a conflict when consumption is attempted, and that attempt does not write a completion disposition. Missing evidence stays unresolved work on the candidate. Restart reads the disposition and the Pending Request. It does not ask a model whether the Goal was probably complete. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

### Acceptance

A worker completion leaves the Goal open. Accepting the last Task review leaves the Goal open. The Coordinator's call to accept completion becomes the Pending Request, and the granting actor is the recorded decider when the captured call executes. The responsible person, or an administrator under the administrator authority ruling, accepts the exact candidate through Pending Requests; the request and the Goal disposition identify the actual deciding actor and never attribute an administrator's decision to the affected person. The Coordinator never approves its own Plan or completion. A second surface cannot close the Goal by a board gesture. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

## Goal Operations

### Definition And Exclusions

Each Goal operation is one semantic operation. A Web action, an internal tool, and a later remote projection call that same operation. The actor and the permission differ. The transport, the catalog shape, and the wire name are not defined here.

The operations are: create Goal, revise intent, create card, edit card, cancel card, propose Plan, approve Plan, cancel Goal, accept completion, and read. Propose Plan raises the approval whose exact intent is one exact Plan version. The Coordinator's accept-completion call becomes the Pending Request whose exact intent is that captured call, because the Coordinator has no authority to accept. Approve Plan and accept completion remain those two operations. No eleventh operation is added. Pause, resume, steering submission, and a private progression tool are not operations. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

### Durable Authority And Projection

The operation commits the Goal-owned records named in its contract, in one commit with the wake marker when the fact is Goal-scoped. Pending Requests owns the approval record. Task Mode owns a Task the Coordinator admits. The read model is a projection of the Goal, its cards, its versions, the linked Threads, and the open Pending Requests. Goal owns semantic operation behavior, not the operation-definition architecture; its surfaces derive from the accepted single definition under [Operation Definition](20261002-operation_definition.md). It does not invent a Goal-only registry.

### Lifecycle

Create Goal records intent, the responsible person, and the Coordinator Thread, and authorizes no worker. Revise intent keeps the Goal and retains the prior intent. An intent revision does not by itself invalidate the active Plan version. Create card, edit card, and cancel card follow the card lifecycle. Propose Plan creates an immutable version, does not activate it, and raises that version's approval. A new proposal invalidates any older open Plan proposal of the same Goal at once, as [Pending Requests](20260930-pending_requests.md) defines, and does not edit the active version. The resolve command for Approve Plan or accept completion records the grant and executes no worker work. The Coordinator's Turn remains the delivery opportunity for the granted intent, and delivery is not itself proof of consumption. The Goal owner consumes a Plan activation or completion grant through the same synchronous re-evaluation-and-claim boundary after its own checks; invalidation that wins before the claim prevents the effect. Consumption of a Plan grant applies the Plan Version eligibility rules and activates the exact approved version through the synchronous re-evaluation-and-claim boundary. Consumption of a completion grant executes the captured accept-completion call, records the granting actor as the decider, and writes the completion disposition. A granted but unconsumed grant of a cancelled Goal is refused with a conflict when consumption is attempted, and that attempt does not activate a Plan version and does not write a completion disposition. Cancel Goal writes the cancel disposition, ends every open Plan approval and completion request of that Goal at once as invalidated with the reason that the Goal was cancelled, and asks the Task owner to interrupt linked running Tasks. Read returns the current records and does not mutate them. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

A handoff from Chat or from Task calls create Goal. The originating Thread keeps a handoff Item. It does not copy its history into the Coordinator Thread.

### Conflict, Missing, Stale, Restart, And Dependency Failure

Every mutating operation names the revision it read, where the record has one, and conflicts on a mismatch. Replay of a completed command returns the stored receipt and does not apply the effect again. A receipt for a deleted Goal stays readable and does not recreate a Goal. A missing Goal after the removal below is a known absence. It is not `recovery_required` for a non-Goal read. Contradictory non-Goal owners still fail through their own owners. Restart uses the durable records and the receipt.

### Acceptance

The person and the Coordinator can each perform create card, edit card, and cancel card, and a stale revision conflicts for both. The consumed Plan grant activates one exact version and does not ask the person to approve the Task again. Accept completion is the only operation that writes the completion disposition. The Coordinator raises that request by calling accept completion, and the call itself does not accept. Read shows the active version unchanged after a later card edit. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

## Coordinator Role

### Definition And Exclusions

The Coordinator is the model role for one Goal. It calls the same operations a person may call, under the same checks. It is not the deterministic Workflow Coordinator, and it is not a new runtime. Its address is the Goal's Thread. Its activity is Turns on that Thread. It has no AgentSession merely because the Goal is long-lived.

### Durable Authority And Projection

The Coordinator decides nothing that the operations do not commit. It does not own Goal truth, a Plan version, or the completion disposition except by calling those operations. The loop that runs the Turn owns no product records. The caller assembles the Turn input from current reads. The worker Context Package is not that input.

### Lifecycle

A Turn is admitted under the wake marker, or to deliver a Pending Request outcome on the Goal's Thread. Inside the active Plan version the Coordinator may select workers, use ordinary methods, order work the version does not order, partition a bounded assignment, request review, incorporate evidence, and admit an ordinary Task. That admission cites the card revision and the Plan version. The Coordinator admits a card whose current revision differs from the revision the active version recorded only when it judges that revision to be within that version's permitted adjustments. Otherwise it proposes a new Plan version instead of admitting. The Coordinator calls accept completion to raise a completion candidate, and that call does not accept. The Coordinator cannot approve its own Plan, write execution status, or turn a worker result into human acceptance. A Turn does not wait for the worker. The next wake admits the later Turn. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

Before requesting completion, the Coordinator inspects its linked Task Threads' Items, referenced Artifact content, terminal outcomes and retained refusal evidence through existing operations reauthorized under the responsible actor, returning only information permitted for the Goal's shared audience and treating missing or refused evidence as unresolved work.

### Conflict, Missing, Stale, Restart, And Dependency Failure

Before each effect the operation rechecks current intent, cancellation, Plan version, and actor. A stale or missing input is the operation's conflict or absence result. It is not repaired from model memory. Restart loses no authority if the provider context is gone. There is one Coordinator Turn at a time on the Goal's Thread because a Thread has one Turn in flight.

### Acceptance

An approved Plan version is enough for the Coordinator to admit a Task without a second human proposal. A change that Plan Version classifies as material raises a new Plan approval instead. A card revision the Coordinator judges to be outside the active version's permitted adjustments raises a new Plan version instead of an admission. A running worker is unchanged by a card edit until the Task owner interrupts it. The decision is recorded in [Goal Contract Gap Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_contract_gap_rulings.md).

## Boundaries

Task Mode owns execution. An authorized Coordinator request is one more source of Task admission. Standalone Task Mode does not gain a loop. Escalation from Task or Chat creates a Goal through create Goal and keeps the originating history where it is.

The scheduler admits worker Turns. It does not wake the Coordinator and does not pin a Sandbox to a Goal. Every worker Turn still takes the scheduler's ordinary authority, environment, context, and execution-attempt checks.

Chat may hand off a long-running outcome, and it may call the Goal intent operations when the person delegates that work. Those calls are the same operations. They are not a second Goal writer.

## Why This Set Stays Small

The following boundaries keep later Business World Model vocabulary from becoming Goal fields. [Goal Redesign Rulings](../decisions/20261002-goal_redesign_rulings.md) records the choice.

A Goal is the outcome and the delegated commitment. Metric, direction, horizon, and guardrail fields are not Goal fields. A guardrail is a rule. The first implementation does not store one on the Goal.

Evidence is an Item, an Artifact, a capability call, an evidence bundle, or a Knowledge Source, when an owner cites it. An Item is communication. It is not evidence until cited. Audit is not Goal progression authority.

An action is a NanoCore operation or a governed external call. Tool use inside a Sandbox stays inside the worker. An operation's effect class is authorization and replay metadata. A claimed business consequence is Knowledge, or later Generative Kernel data. The two are not the same fact.

Controls are the Policy Kernel, the Sandbox, the Gateway, the vault, and Pending Request approvals. Business rules are Knowledge. Typed business entities belong to the Generative Kernel when that layer is used. The first implementation has no entity registry and no convention compiler.

The worker's pushed context is the Context Package. The Coordinator's context is the assembled Turn input plus reads. Knowledge retrieval uses the read Task preparation already uses. Publishing a convention, a Skill, a manifest default, or a generated view stays with that owner. The first implementation does none of those writes.

## Retained Data Removal

The new implementation and the removal of existing Goal-owned data land together. The removal is one-way. There is no legacy reader and no dual write. Dropping the old rows first would make non-Goal readers fail closed before they can treat a missing Goal as a known absence.

The removal drops the Goal record, the Goal Plan record, the Goal task record, the Goal review record, the Goal verification record, the pending user-turn record, and the steering terminal-outcome record. Those stores are Goal-owned. Their only purpose is the retired contract.

Threads, Turns, Items, Artifacts, audit events, evidence bundles, runtime evidence, worker-storage contributor rows, and command receipts stay. A Goal reference on a shared row stays as lineage and does not require a live Goal. The absent Task id and binding-only selection rules govern new admissions. Retained Turn-owned traces and package bytes remain usable as historical evidence. References to deleted Goal-owned rows confer no authority and require no live Goal or Goal Task during historical validation or import. A protocol `plan` Item stays because Item history is retained. Worker-turn checkpoints that belong to a Goal are closed or dropped so recovery does not resume a deleted Goal. Checkpoints with no Goal id stay. The Sandbox pin field is cleared and then dropped with the scheduler owner's removal of that policy.

A historical receipt stays readable. Replay does not recreate a Goal. A missing Goal is a known absence for non-Goal reads.

## Current Implementation Projection

The ten operations are defined in `packages/app-api-schemas/src/goal.ts` and composed into `PRODUCT_OPERATION_DEFINITIONS`. NanoCore invocation joins them to `runtime/goal-owner.ts`; HTTP, OpenAPI, Core Client, CLI and Coordinator Tools derive from the same family. The joined read includes current intent/history, cards, exact immutable Plan versions, shared Pending Request resolution and claim, linked ordinary Task states and disposition.

Workspace SQL stores `goals`, `goal_cards`, `goal_plan_versions` and `goal_card_tasks`. Goal commands commit the change revision with their fact. `task_turn_terminal_facts` is a minimal Task-owned terminal fact that commits with the linked Goal revision before file publication; boot republishes the terminal from that fact and considers open Goals. `runtime/goal-coordinator.ts` uses ordinary internal-agent Turns without an AgentSession through the existing loop and Gateway; current `goal-orchestrator` role configuration remains usable.

Human Plan and completion decisions resolve shared Pending Requests; the Coordinator consumes an eligible grant through synchronous current-authority re-evaluation and claim. Exact Plan activation restores neither intent nor cards and starts no workers. Ordinary Task reservation checks the current intent and card cancellation and records the active Plan and admission card revision, with the Coordinator's permitted-adjustment judgment.

Automatic Assistant Goal handoff refuses a private source Thread or selected private Artifact body before publishing a shared Goal. Explicit Goal commands and shared-source handoffs remain available through the existing entry paths.

Storage migration drops `goal_records`, `goal_plan_records`, `goal_tasks`, `goal_review_records`, `goal_verification_records`, `pending_user_turn_records` and `steering_terminal_outcomes`, drops Goal-owned checkpoints, and clears and drops `sandbox_runtime_records.pinned_goal_id`. Shared history, evidence, audit, contributors, ordinary checkpoints and receipts remain. Workspace export/import carries the current records without importing approval grants or restoring retired Goal authority.
