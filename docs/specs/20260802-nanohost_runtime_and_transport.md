# NanoHost Runtime And Transport

This specification is the single entry owner of the NanoHost execution-host topology, identity and placement definitions, component boundaries, shared-Sandbox and AgentSession lifecycle, compatibility keys, on-demand idle Sandbox retirement, operator distribution/installation/replacement/inspection/image-store surface, and Runtime Epoch fail-stop recovery and forensic reporting.

## Scope

### Owns

- The implementation-facing definition, lifecycle, failure boundary, recovery, and readiness contract for one configured NanoHost and its private Runtime Epoch, including shared-Sandbox/Harness/AgentSession residency (`SandboxCompatibilityKey`, `HarnessCompatibilityKey`, `AgentSessionCompatibilityKey`), on-demand idle Sandbox retirement, the epoch-external NanoHost Image Store and local acquisition/build/import, target-matched distribution/installation/replacement/live inspection, the Epoch Invalidation Report, and the Epoch rebuild cost budget and falsifiable premise.

- The NanoCore-to-NanoHost and nested HTTP/2 session carriage, transport credentials (`nanohost-transport`) and TLS trust, fixed effect and Harness envelopes, stream/flow-control reservations, liveness and timer bounds, and predecessor fencing in [NanoHost Transport](20261116-nanohost_transport.md) (`docs/specs/20261116-nanohost_transport.md`), and the NanoHost-to-Gateway loopback SDK boundary, stock `ForwardTcp`/`RelayOpen`/`ConnectSupervisor`/`RelayStream` Sandbox bridge, fixed unary worker bootstrap and single-file helper realization, epoch-private network and mount namespace containment, and stock OpenShell release qualification in [NanoHost OpenShell Gateway](20261116-nanohost_openshell_gateway.md) (`docs/specs/20261116-nanohost_openshell_gateway.md`).
- Scope execution intent and outcome transactions, global reservation commit gaps, and deadline progression inside NanoCore in [Durable Execution And Control Store](20261019-durable_execution_and_control_store.md) ([decision](../decisions/20261019-durable_execution_and_control_store.md)), and scheduler admission, claim selection, execution-attempt grants, and capacity accounting in [Durable Scheduler Design](20260703-durable_scheduler_design.md) and [Runtime Scheduling Scale](20260703-durable_scheduler_design.md#scale-limits).

## Definitions

- **`Execution Host` and `NanoHost`**: `Execution Host` is the generic deployment role; `NanoHost` is OpenKit's sole concrete product and implementation of that role. One deployment NanoHost owns one current private Runtime Epoch containing the NanoHost service, one stock OpenShell Gateway, the effect-capable members of one configured container backend, and zero or more Sandboxes. NanoHost is the sole local OpenShell lifecycle authority, replacing per-AgentSession disposable Cells.
- **`NanoHost service`**: the trusted execution-host process (`apps/nanohost`) that owns local OpenShell lifecycle effects, the Runtime Epoch, transport projection, package materialization, Sandbox creation and deletion, Sandbox Integration attachment, evidence transfer, and cleanup reporting. It applies only already-authorized work selected by NanoCore's scheduler and policy owners and never selects product work, broadens permission, creates product state, settles uncertain external effects, applies Workspace changes, or infers Turn completion.
- **`Runtime Epoch`**: the private lifecycle, readiness, and failure boundary for one generation of epoch-local execution infrastructure. Runtime Epoch identity is private execution evidence and MUST NOT become a Core protocol record, scheduling identity, user-visible lifecycle, Workspace truth, or substitute for AgentSession identity. The server-admin `nanohost.runtime-target` read may project the reported `physicalEpoch` as nullable stored correlation under [Live Operator Inspection](#live-operator-inspection) without granting runtime, placement, scheduling, or host authority or adding public machine or executable observation fields.
- **`Effect-capable member`**: any process or service in the epoch that can accept, retain, forward, complete, or materialize an OpenShell or container-backend effect, comprising the NanoHost service, the stock Gateway, every member of the configured container backend (`dockerd`, `containerd`, and their helpers and proxies, plus the epoch `slirp4netns` helper), and sandbox execution descendants. Monitoring, logging, or read-only diagnostic processes may remain outside the failure group only when they cannot signal, forward, mutate, authenticate to, or complete a runtime effect. The OS service manager's control-group termination supplies the fail-stop guarantee rather than any implementation language; this specification makes no claim that other languages cannot express the required transport or process mechanisms.
- **`Sandbox Integration`**: the sandbox-local runtime supervisor (`packages/worker-shim`) that adapts a worker Agent or harness to the local worker-control, inference, capability, policy, provider, and runtime bindings supplied through NanoHost and stock OpenShell. It owns only outer transport adaptation and sandbox-local injection of already-governed runtime context, never choosing work, models, provider fallback, permissions, capabilities, tools, rate limits, usage, Audit, terminal outcomes, or Workspace publication.
- **`Private residency records and measured image identity`**: `SandboxRuntimeRecord`, `HarnessInstanceRecord`, and `AgentSessionRuntimeBinding` are private runtime residency projections inside the NanoCore-process NanoHost backend adapter, not product records, new durable workflow owners, App API identities, authorization inputs, Workspace truth, or ordinary diagnostics. Idle AgentSessions consume open-session capacity but hold no execution attempt or effect authority. Measured harness identity is the Sandbox's verified `image_digest` copied by value at binding time for both new and reused bindings, never unverified authored AEP `agent.runtimeVersion` or `runtime.image` references. Image-digest grouping may false-split when a harness receives a new base image, MUST NOT false-merge when different binaries carry the same author label, and cannot claim which harness binary version ran without a verified image-to-binary map.

## Components And Placement

```text
Runtime Epoch
├── NanoHost service (apps/nanohost)
├── stock OpenShell Gateway (foreground process)
├── configured container backend (V1: dedicated dockerd, containerd, and slirp4netns)
└── zero or more Sandboxes and AgentSession executions

outside every epoch, durable and inert
├── NanoHost Image Store (/var/lib/openkit/nanohost-images)
├── Persistent Worker Volumes (/var/lib/openkit/nanohost-work)
└── NanoHost Token slots and forensic evidence
```

- **Technical role matrix and single binary boundary**: the NanoHost service is one deployable Rust binary crate at `apps/nanohost` (`@openkit/nanohost`, not a shared `packages/` library, with no parallel Go or TypeScript service implementation, no in-process Gateway server, no normal-operation OpenShell CLI subprocess wrapper, and no vendored protobuf or generated-client fallback). It compiles the official `openshell-sdk` from the Cargo-locked release commit and runs the checksum-verified stock Gateway as a separate foreground process. The same one-NanoHost topology operates when NanoCore is co-located, containerized, or on another host without changing ownership or authentication. NanoCore neither prepares, creates, deletes, inspects, nor recycles OpenShell resources and holds no Cell, SSH lifecycle, remote Gateway, or Docker endpoint handle.

| Role | V1 realization | Owns | Excludes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NanoHost service | One Rust binary crate under `apps/nanohost` | Runtime Epoch coordination, one NanoCore session, one Gateway client channel, local OpenShell effects, sandbox bridges, readiness, and bounded evidence. | Product authority, scheduler policy, durable operation recovery, alternate language implementation, and arbitrary proxying. |
| OpenShell client boundary | Official `openshell-sdk` source fixed to the supported release commit by Cargo and `Cargo.lock` | Typed lifecycle and forwarding RPC access over one authenticated loopback gRPC/HTTP/2 channel. | Vendored protobuf copies, generated-client fallback, Gateway server code, CLI output parsing, filesystem discovery, floating `main`, and OpenShell protocol extensions. |
| stock OpenShell Gateway | Official unmodified checksum-verified `v0.0.99` foreground executable | Stock Gateway state, authentication, sandbox lifecycle coordination, `ForwardTcp`, Supervisor pairing, and byte relay. | OpenKit route semantics, NanoCore connectivity, in-process linking, OpenKit patches, and independent restart. |
| configured container backend | One dedicated `dockerd` and its dedicated `containerd` with NanoHost-private roots, sockets, and networks, as foreground service-cgroup members | Container and sandbox execution state for the current epoch only. | Backend selection, image acquisition, product authority, membership in more than one epoch, independent restart, and any effect domain outside the NanoHost service cgroup. |
| NanoHost Image Store | One durable epoch-external digest-addressed content directory plus a bounded index, owned by the NanoHost service account | Verified inert image content and its local index. | Registry service behavior, published artifacts, product records, canonical storage, mutable runtime state, credentials, listeners, and epoch membership. |
| OS supervisor | Linux service manager and cgroup boundary | Complete group kill, host-to-Sandbox process containment, fresh-epoch restart, and proof that no effect-capable member survives NanoHost failure. | Member-local recovery, a repository-owned supervisor framework, and readiness adjudication. |
| Sandbox Integration | Existing TypeScript `packages/worker-shim` evolved in place | Sandbox-local standard HTTP/2 client, fixed local binding, governed runtime-context injection, and Worker Agent process supervision. | Gateway credentials, `ForwardTcp`, direct NanoCore connectivity, route authority, and a second shim package. |
| NanoCore | Existing TypeScript `apps/nanocore` application | Durable work, scheduler, identity, permission, route semantics, lineage, sequence, usage, audit, product state, and authorized build definitions supplied as backend build input. | Gateway, container-backend, sandbox, process, image-store, or Runtime Epoch lifecycle effects, and any host instruction. |

- **Internal roles, Rust stack, and reversion condition**: `apps/nanohost` has four logical internal responsibility roles (epoch coordinator, NanoCore-session owner, OpenShell-client owner, and per-Sandbox bridge owner) inside one binary crate rather than public interfaces, separate crates, plugins, services, databases, or durable state machines; source module count is not contractual. It uses a small pinned async stack (Tokio for process and I/O ownership, the official exact-release OpenShell SDK for Gateway gRPC, `h2` for the server side of the nested sandbox HTTP/2 session, and Rustls for non-loopback NanoCore TLS) under the app-local Cargo lock and toolchain owner (`docs/toolchain.md`, `docs/cookbooks/rust-setup.md`), with no application Web framework or copied Rust protocol schema family. Rust (`apps/nanohost`) is the sole selectable NanoHost implementation for self-contained execution-host deployment, predictable low resident footprint beside the container backend, direct reuse of the upstream Rust OpenShell SDK, and explicit HTTP/2 flow-control management; the decision reverts to a TypeScript implementation (keeping the stock Gateway as a separate checksum-verified foreground process and without weakening the contract, forking OpenShell, or splitting NanoHost across two languages) only if the official SDK client boundary cannot invoke required stock operations, the transport envelope cannot be met in the Rust stack, or delegated implementation and review throughput proves materially worse than the equivalent TypeScript path on comparable work.
- **Scale, authority, backend, and retired-topology boundaries**: under the substrate doctrine in [Runtime Model](../core/runtime-model.md) (NanoHost collects execution facts while NanoCore accepts product facts, work descends while authority does not, bytes move while truth does not, and NanoHost buffers only what it produces without caching what it was granted) and [Durable Execution And Control Store](20261019-durable_execution_and_control_store.md) (which lives inside NanoCore without adding a NanoHost operation journal, Worker inbox, or recovery service, while existing physical placement, Epoch, attachment, identity, command-receipt, current-authority, and cleanup proofs remain binding until individually proved ablations remove them), NanoCore retains all decision authority and NanoHost applies only already-authorized work. The current implementation stays inside the small-deployment profile in [Runtime Scheduling Scale](20260703-durable_scheduler_design.md#scale-limits) with one configured NanoHost and one configured host-local container backend; no product-visible NanoHost, Runtime Epoch, Gateway, container, network, process, or backend-native record exists, and dynamic discovery, fleet scheduling, multi-NanoHost placement, live AgentSession migration, active-active execution, high availability, warm pools, and remote attestation are excluded unless a separate accepted scale/attestation architecture is adopted. The legacy NanoCore-owned per-Session OpenShell Cell lifecycle and configuration are removed and are never a selectable fallback or second lifecycle owner. Configuration and execution exclude any Cell owner, Cell epoch, Cell helper, NanoCore-to-SSH lifecycle target, NanoCore-to-Gateway URL, operator-managed Gateway forward, direct Sandbox-to-NanoCore endpoint, custom OpenShell binary, or TLS-verification bypass; SSH may be used only for installation-time stdin secret-sink delivery, diagnostics, evidence collection, or human-authorized break-glass response (aside from the pinned opaque Gateway-internal single-file helper relay in [NanoHost OpenShell Gateway](20261116-nanohost_openshell_gateway.md)), never for normal execution or recovery. NanoHost introduces no durable operation journal, settlement coordinator, recovery state machine, database, actor framework, plugin framework, multi-crate workspace, in-process Gateway server, or normal-operation OpenShell CLI wrapper.
- **Epoch boundary and foreground cgroup supervision**: the Runtime Epoch includes every mutable root, socket, network, cgroup, process, authentication material, and backend handle that could complete an accepted OpenShell effect. On the V1 Linux execution host, NanoHost starts the fixed stock Gateway and dedicated container-backend executables once as foreground children inside its OS service cgroup using direct argument vectors and fixed NanoHost-owned paths without a shell or wrapper script, under mandatory control-group termination semantics equivalent to systemd `KillMode=control-group` and with no OpenKit supervisor framework.
- **Configured container backend and effect-domain classification**: NanoHost instantiates, provisions private roots for, proves empty, and rebuilds only the single configured container backend; supporting additional backends is a configuration-time selection concern only (with no backend abstraction layer, driver framework, capability negotiation, or live runtime switch), changing the configured backend replaces the epoch through full invalidation and fresh creation, and an epoch that starts an unconfigured backend is invalid. Every candidate backend is classified by its effect domain before configuration:

| Class | Meaning | Fence | V1 status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Host-local effect domain | Every process able to accept, retain, forward, complete, or materialize an accepted sandbox effect is a descendant member of the NanoHost service cgroup on the execution host. | Complete control-group termination proves the prior effect domain impossible. | Docker authorized (`dockerd` + `containerd`); Podman requires its own accepted realization contract. |
| External effect domain | An accepted effect can be completed by a control plane, scheduler, kubelet, or node agent outside the NanoHost process tree and cgroup. | Cgroup termination is **not** a fence; a generation-scoped external fencing contract is required. | Kubernetes and every other remote orchestrator are **not authorized**. |

- **Epoch-external Image Store and Persistent Worker Volumes**: the NanoHost Image Store (`/var/lib/openkit/nanohost-images`) and the NanoHost Token sink remain outside every Runtime Epoch and MUST NOT contain mutable runtime state, live sockets, process identity, Gateway data, container records, or epoch authentication authority. The Image Store is inert local supply (never a registry, published artifact, product record, canonical storage, or network service) holding only verified digest-addressed OCI manifests, configs, and layer blobs plus a bounded local index readable and writable only by the NanoHost service account, without certifying user-authored blobs as credential-free; the configured container backend receives images only through explicit NanoHost-driven import, never by sharing or mounting the store. Published worker images are never a prerequisite for NanoHost startup or readiness: fresh Epoch creation starts the private backend and Gateway without acquiring, building, or importing Worker images and without `OPENKIT_NANOHOST_REQUIRED_IMAGE_DIGESTS`, a startup image list/count, or a pre-ready effect lane (while stock OpenShell Supervisor bootstrap may access GHCR before Gateway health). Under [Persistent Worker Volumes](20260910-persistent_worker_volumes.md), NanoHost alone derives root-validated fixed mounts through stock `SandboxTemplate.driver_config` and the private Docker driver's explicit mount support (callers never supply backend paths or arbitrary driver config), and `/var/lib/openkit/nanohost-work` is a durable execution-host root outside epoch roots, the Image Store, credentials, and forensic evidence that is never removed by recursive epoch cleanup, explicit image removal, or normal Sandbox deletion.

## Sandbox And Session Lifecycle

### Shared-Sandbox Harness Topology

```text
Runtime Epoch
└── SandboxRuntimeRecord
    ├── HarnessInstanceRecord A (one runtime and static configuration)
    │   ├── AgentSessionRuntimeBinding 1
    │   └── AgentSessionRuntimeBinding 2..N
    └── HarnessInstanceRecord B..N
        └── AgentSessionRuntimeBinding 1..N

Turn
└── ExecutionLease + AEP snapshot + Context Package + route lineage
```

- **Residency records and capacity bounds**: one compatible Sandbox may host multiple declared compatibility-keyed Harnesses (`maxHarnesses`) and multiple open AgentSessions for distinct Threads (`maxOpenSessions`), with bounded concurrent active Turns across distinct AgentSessions (`maxActiveTurns`, initial default `20`, with `maxOpenSessions` at least `max(8, maxActiveTurns)`). AgentSession binding is independent of Turn execution-attempt grants and creates no extra public runtime identity, product session, second scheduler, or independent capacity authority ([decision](../decisions/20261006-execution_backend_port_in_nanocore.md)). `SandboxRuntimeRecord` owns the opaque NanoHost Sandbox binding, `SandboxCompatibilityKey`, static environment class, declared Harness set, `maxHarnesses`, aggregate capacity, lifecycle/health/drain state, cleanup evidence, and measured `image_digest` (copied by value at binding time for both new and reused bindings because `sandbox_runtime_records` rows are deleted with the physical Sandbox in `removeNanoHostSandboxRuntimeForHarness` and `removeNanoHostSandboxRuntimeByBinding` in `apps/nanocore/src/runtime/nanohost-harness-records.ts`). `HarnessInstanceRecord` binds the owning Sandbox binding, exact `HarnessCompatibilityKey`, runtime and adapter family and version, protocol, bounded product-safe capabilities, `maxOpenSessions`, `maxActiveTurns`, occupancy, liveness, and drain state. `AgentSessionRuntimeBinding` maps one exact current Core AgentSession to that Harness, its exact Thread, one restricted native conversation handle, one `AgentSessionCompatibilityKey`, and current lifecycle proof.
- **Supervisor, resident native processes, and isolation claims**: Sandbox Integration is one long-lived supervisor per Sandbox that establishes the private Harness-control pull path before any AgentSession opens, retains only bounded current-operation state, and supervises one resident native Agent process per active AgentSession across sequential Turns until `session.close` releases it, while a successor AgentSession resumes the exact retained native conversation ([AgentSession](../core/agent-session.md)). Runtime-native sub-agents, child threads, or Provider sessions beneath one governed AgentSession remain private runtime activity without creating Core identity, capacity, or transport authority, and Harness readiness alone creates no AgentSession, Turn, route token, native handle, or child process. Process identity is neither stored in Core nor part of a compatibility key. An adapter that creates its native conversation only with the first prompt may hold one open binding with `nativeHandleState=pending`, and its first successful Turn must establish the handle digest before that binding returns to reusable idle. Sibling AgentSessions belong to distinct Threads and always keep distinct private state roots, native handles, child processes, Turn slots, route credentials, and cleanup proofs; a predecessor for a Thread retains no resident binding after its successor becomes current. A Harness may execute concurrent active Turns only when its fixed `maxActiveTurns > 1` and its adapter proves independent multiplexing, routing, interruption, context, output, credential binding, and cleanup; each AgentSession and Thread still has at most one active Turn, and a Harness never selects work, grants capacity, extends deadlines, chooses logical models, broadens authority, applies Workspace changes, or infers terminal state. Shared-Sandbox co-residency claims only **conversation-context isolation** (when the adapter proves native context, prompt/tool state, sequence, interruption, transcript, and route targeting remain AgentSession-specific) and **Workspace-write isolation** (through separate AgentSession mutable slots and conflict-checked apply); it claims no **security and adjudication isolation** by default because the Sandbox and Harness share one compromise domain, so adversarial work, independent adjudication, incompatible trust or credential visibility, authorization-sensitive work, or strict-risk work requires OS isolation or a separate Sandbox.
- **Three compatibility decisions**: placement and reuse evaluate three distinct immutable keys recorded at binding time (with the measured image digest copied by value) and re-evaluated on every placement, reuse, or restart decision:
  1. `SandboxCompatibilityKey` decides whether AgentSessions may share a Sandbox and matches both the exact selected storage association and the static Sandbox envelope (Workspace, admitted trust class, Sandbox image, Sandbox Integration version, declared Harness set, OS user/group posture, filesystem and mount envelope, network policy, provider attachment visibility, Vault injection visibility class, static credential exposure class, resource class, backend capability summary, sensitivity class, and containment policy), excluding Turn payloads, Thread history, native handles, raw secrets, temporary uploads, output contents, worker-private caches, and short-lived authority. Equal images or keys never merge distinct named Sandboxes; the first association CAS reserves one physical attachment generation while a second eligible member's distinct Thread joins through contributor admission and its own work slot and binding ([Persistent Worker Volumes](20260910-persistent_worker_volumes.md), [User-Selected Workspace-Shared Sandboxes](../decisions/20261008-user_selected_workspace_shared_sandboxes.md)). Stale races require a fresh selection/request without silent rebasing, and unknown attachment or teardown blocks another writer or unproved reuse.
  2. `HarnessCompatibilityKey` decides whether an AgentSession may reuse an existing Harness Instance inside that Sandbox and is derived once from the resolved static Harness descriptor (runtime family, adapter and native-runtime version, Harness protocol, runtime binary and state-root layout, fixed plugin/extension/hook/runtime-config digests, fixed process-environment posture, declared product-safe capabilities, Harness resource class, and verified static Skill/MCP worker-supply projection in `apps/nanocore/src/runtime/nanohost-compatibility.ts:189`), excluding Workspace, AgentSession, Thread, Turn, logical-model choice, transient Context Package, child-only public native environment, raw secrets, per-Turn route tokens, native handle, and process identity.
  3. `AgentSessionCompatibilityKey` decides whether a later Turn may reuse an existing native conversation continuity and includes Agent and profile identity, Thread affinity, native conversation protocol, AgentSession workspace layout ([Session Static Workspace Materialization](20260704-session_static_workspace_materialization.md)), context and transcript posture, static policy envelope, provider-session posture, required features, and effective child-only public environment plus its verified image-default identity (reuse also rechecks the nested `HarnessCompatibilityKey` in `apps/nanocore/src/runtime/nanohost-harness-records.ts:508` and `turn-executor-factory.ts:2660`). Ordinary materializable Turn input revisions do not invalidate it; a changed child-only public environment map or changed Skill/MCP supply selection rejects in-place reuse and applies through an authorized successor on the next Turn with exact native resume rather than mutating a resident process or active session files, while a changed static path, shared process environment, OS user, provider posture, network envelope, credential visibility class, or required Harness feature drains or replaces the wider scope, and credential revocation interrupts and closes immediately.
- **Epoch creation, immutable membership, and readiness order**: on first start or recovery, the supervisor establishes a new group and NanoHost creates fresh private roots, sockets, networks, namespace state, authentication material, container-runtime state, and Gateway state, validating identity, stock component identities, Supervisor ownership, private root ownership, loopback Gateway binding, host-to-Sandbox isolation, and absence of stale effect-capable processes before accepting work (failed creation keeps NanoHost non-ready, exports one `epoch-creation-failure` report, terminates the partial group, and retries only through a fresh epoch). Epoch identity, component identities, configured backend, mutable roots, sockets, networks, namespaces, supervision membership, and epoch-local authentication generation are immutable; any change replaces the epoch through full invalidation and fresh creation, while NanoHost Token slot updates remain outside epoch identity and only Sandbox/Harness create/delete and point-of-use image import mutate membership inside a healthy epoch. `EpochCoordinator::start` proves the epoch's slot fresh and unoccupied before any effect; if NanoCore is unavailable, NanoHost stays locally healthy without advertising capacity until `EpochCoordinator::start`, physical admission, and durable empty `204` readiness acknowledgement complete in order before the first effect poll, and a healthy reconnect repeats admission and readiness without rebuilding the still-live epoch while `RuntimeTarget` preserves any occupied or cleanup-held slot.
### Persistent Worker Storage Amendment

- **Admission, `sandbox.create` storage attachment, and per-Turn materialization**: NanoCore owns public-endpoint classification, current-authority admission ([decision](../decisions/20260930-public_endpoints_by_admitted_grant.md)), and persisted scheduler attempt authority before NanoHost receives work; only a bounded `200` response on a fixed effect command path admits that local operation (`204` admits nothing, with no local command queue or authority cache). For `sandbox.create`, `storageRef` is a nonempty opaque JSON string of at most 512 UTF-8 bytes with no Unicode control characters or leading/trailing Unicode whitespace, `scopeDigest` is `sha256:` followed by 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, `attachmentGeneration` is a JSON integer from `1` through `9,007,199,254,740,991`, and `sandboxId` is a nonempty JSON string of at most 512 UTF-8 bytes with no Unicode control characters; these grammars confer no attachment authority. NanoHost verifies root identity and ownership, initializes only explicitly new volumes from the exact image, and supplies fixed validated mounts to the official SDK (root metadata is host-owned and not worker-writable, definite Sandbox deletion releases the attachment without purging volume data, and image builds never mount retained work). `workspace.collect` ([NanoHost Workspace Data Boundary](20260801-nanohost_workspace_data_boundary.md)) runs only against the current live Sandbox and matching retained attachment verified through the epoch and volume owners before scanning, neither changes generation nor releases the attachment, and precedes teardown when required at release. When no compatible ready Sandbox and declared Harness set exist, NanoHost runs stock Provider setup, Sandbox creation, static materialization, Sandbox Integration launch, bridge establishment, and the first private Harness poll before settling `bridge.open`; for a later compatible AgentSession, NanoCore grants open-session capacity and delivers `session.open` with the selected `harnessInstanceId` without recreating the Sandbox, bridge, Integration, or Harness. Every `turn.start` receives a fresh immutable per-Turn AEP snapshot, Context Package, execution attempt, deadline, and sequence scope bound to its exact owner lineage, delivering Turn route tokens only to Sandbox Integration while the resident process keeps its `session.open` loopback credentials; no Sandbox-wide AEP or credential package exists, and missing, stale, revoked, conflicting, or dependency-failed Turn inputs block `turn.start`. Verified AEP import precedes worker-supply imports ([Agent Plugin Packaging And Worker Supply](20260907-agent_plugin_packaging_and_worker_supply.md), producing a read-only Session-private native projection of selected Skills and the fixed Gateway MCP binding without original MCP config, vendor extensions, package fetch, version choice, or new routes, and blocking readiness if exact adapter/image/flag support is unproved), which precede Context Package imports and `turn.start` (for the first open, initial AEP and session-static worker supply import before `session.open`, and for a newly initialized work slot NanoCore resolves [Workspace Synchronization](20260703-workspace_synchronization.md) check values and completes durable baseline collection after `session.open` before the first `turn.start` under [decision](../decisions/20260930-first_accepted_base_by_baseline_scan.md), whereas a retained slot is not re-baselined).

### Replacement Close Lifecycle

This section owns the decision points of one **local close** of an idle predecessor binding. The close comes from a successor replacement or from an authorized release of the retained binding. The product rules for close, drain and on-demand idle retirement are in [Persistent Worker Storage Amendment](#persistent-worker-storage-amendment). The scheduler holds, the release edges and the E7 reread are in [Main Selection Handoff](20260703-durable_scheduler_design.md#main-selection-handoff), in particular Holds And Release Edges and invariants I5, I6 and I7. A Login close uses the same Main slots and keeps its own departure ACK and fence contract.

- **One lifecycle owner and the existing deciders.** One in-process owner in Main drives each close from selection to E7, on the live path and on the cold path. A pure `decide(facts, event)` returns the next action and the proof that the action needs. The owner gives each action to its deciding writer. That writer decides in its own transaction or exact readback:
  - Workspace decides the capture claim, publication, recovery and abandon.
  - Core decides the attempt, request authority, the idle-binding proof and receipt consumption.
  - Native decides the binding, Harness slot, latch, queue, dispatch, settle, restore and removal.
  - Transport decides the synchronous write of the first command bytes to the bound HTTP/2 response writer.
  - Main decides its admission-visible slots (the pending Harness operation and the close owner), its scheduler marks and the E7 request.
- **Cold path.** The cold path calls the same `decide` only with facts that it reads again from these owners. The capacity predicate only reads. The lifecycle adds no store, worker, timer, retry loop or receipt. ENGINEER option A (11-25) adds one retained field and one private IPC arm, both additive: the claim generation on the Harness record (G3) and `harness.claim {revision}` in `sandbox.restore-admission`. Existing rows read the generation as null (legacy). A downgrade fails closed: exact journal admission applies to every server-owner journal, so an older binary finds the newer journal entry outside its own journal and refuses the file. The additive column leaves every baseline column and value unchanged, and the rollback source is the pre-upgrade copy that the offline owner migration (`migrateServerOwner`) preserves. A mixed local Main and Worker pair fails closed. A draining row with a null generation stays fenced at the cold exit, because its claim history stays ambiguous.

**Clocks.** Four clocks run independently. No lock or wait stops a clock. No poll, reopen or retry restarts a clock.

- **Tc, the close allowance**, starts at the original enqueue. Main checks it at the actual post with the wall clock and the monotonic clock. The first carriage checks it again. The original enqueue wall instant is the lower edge of Tc. A wall sample before it comes from a wall step back, and no deadline can be read from it. Main refuses such a sample at RC7 and at RC10 with `close-allowance`. Native has no lower edge: after a step back before RC8 the queue can commit, and RC10 then sends zero bytes. A step back that stays after the enqueue instant is an existing clock limit.
- **Ts, the successor startup deadline.** Main checks it at the actual post. The first carriage checks it again.
- **Ta, the authority expiry.** Main folds the cutoffs of the Ta row into one clock. Main binds the slot under the drained RC6 Core hold, or verifies the prepared minimum there and refuses to post a stale slot. The wiring verifies: RC7 reads every cutoff again under the drained hold, and a cutoff earlier than the prepared slot fails R, so the post refuses with `request`. Main checks Ta at the actual post. The first carriage checks it again and honors a shorter cutoff that a rotation of an original credential introduced after the post. An acknowledged deadline refusal still maps from the posted clock. A missing, revoked, mismatched or invalid credential stays under R. An elapsed known cutoff keeps `authority-expired`.
- **The deciding queue cutoff.** Native compares one absolute wall value inside the queue transaction, because a monotonic instant does not compare across workers or restarts. Main puts the earliest of Tc, Ts and Ta in the existing private replacement deadline slot. The queue frame gets no new field. Main keeps each original clock and the limiting cause, so that the typed refusal, the P14 order, the monotonic checks and W stay the same.
- **The original cause.** Native refuses an expired slot with its one deadline refusal. When Main posts the slot, it records which clock gave the minimum. Main maps an acknowledged deadline refusal to the cause of that recorded clock. It does not read the wall clock again, so a wall step back between the refusal and the ACK does not change the cause. Example: Ta is 100, Ts is 200 and Tc is 300. Native refuses at wall 101, and the wall steps back to 99. The answer is still `authority-expired`. The answer is `authority-expired`, `startup-deadline` or `close-allowance`, never one fixed cause. Each clock refusal at RC7 and RC10 answers the limiting clock, which is the earliest original deadline that has passed. A clock has passed when its wall deadline passed at the sample, when a read carried its expiry (H4-1), or, for Tc, when the monotonic check failed. A failed monotonic check therefore puts Tc in the minimum, not only at the lower edge. Example: the close is admitted at 50 with Tc 90, Ta 100 and Ts 200. R sees 101 and carries an expired Ta. The final wall sample reads 89. With the monotonic Tc expired, the answer is `close-allowance` with zero frames at RC7 or zero bytes at RC10 and the exact unsent settlement. With the monotonic Tc live, the answer is `authority-expired`. A sample before the original enqueue instant keeps the lower-edge rule and answers the limiting clock. At RC7 and RC10 the clocks come before R and the P14 cause, because the P14 owner also refuses an elapsed Ts and the R reader an elapsed credential. A genuine audience or storage denial with live clocks keeps the P14 cause.
- **The sample after the last read.** A clock can pass, or the wall can step back, between any two fact reads of an effect and between its last read and the effect. So an effect that a clock decides takes its clock sample after its last successful fact read, or compares inside its deciding write. RC7 and RC10 gather every fact first, including the exact dispatched key at RC10, then sample the wall and monotonic clocks and decide in the same synchronous run as the post or the first bytes. RC8 compares the slot inside the deciding queue update, after the target, binding, storage and prepared-key reads, and keeps the typed deadline refusal and the posted clock. A Native wall sample after the reads but before that update is stale: a deadline that passes between the two must leave zero rows. The P14 read checks Ts itself, so an elapsed Ts before that read refuses there. The R read checks the credential itself, issuer grace included. An R refusal from an elapsed credential answers the limiting clock (`authority-expired`), never `request`; a revocation on a live clock answers `request`. The read carries the clock that it saw elapse to the decision: the R read carries an elapsed credential (expired, or rotated past its grace), and the P14 read an elapsed Ts. So the wall can step back after that read and before the sample, and the decision still answers the clock cause with zero frames or zero bytes (H4-1). RC10 checks the smaller of the posted Ta and the Ta that it folds from the current cutoffs, because a rotation after the post can only shorten Ta (H4-2). The R read and this fold read the same rotation independently; each alone keeps `authority-expired`.
- **The monotonic Tc** is independent of the wall Tc. It can fail while the wall Tc is live. Main then refuses with `close-allowance` at RC7, or RC10 sends zero bytes. Native compares only the wall slot.
- **W, the caller bound**, rejects the caller with its typed cause at the original deadline. It does not wait for a writer reply and it gives no release. The owner keeps both slots and the writer outcome until proof.

If Tc, Ts or Ta expires before the deciding queue commit, the result is zero queued rows and zero close bytes. If one of them expires after the queue commit and before the first bytes, the result is zero close bytes. Each expiry is tested with the other limits live. The queue timestamp, command bytes, fingerprint and receipt stay the same. A cold reader uses only absolute wall values.

**Pins.** The Core hold pins R, B and the applied Workspace policy. The successor Workspace hold pins the Thread rows of A. Both pin from RC6 until the RC8 reply reaches Main. The policy publisher gets its activation ACK or readback without the hold. Only its swap waits. If the worker can still commit, Main joins the worker exit before it reopens. A commit whose reply has not reached Main still holds both pins. A stopped producer holds no pin, and its commit goes to the cold path (P4). At first carriage the poll owner holds the pins through the synchronous write. On a definite refusal it reserves the unsent delivery against other polls, releases the short pins, and then calls the settlement owner, which acquires them again. Pins release in `finally`. A pin never outlives the decision that it serves. A caller bound never releases a pin and never waits for one.

**Unknown outcomes.** An unknown outcome is not unsent, and it is not a noncommit until an owner proves it.

- **RC7 or RC8 reply lost.** Join writer quiescence, then read the original slot, id and sequence. A later queue can reuse the Harness cell, so writer quiescence and a different current key do not prove that the original never committed. Absence is proved only when the cell is idle or settled and its next sequence is still the original sequence, or when the exact receipt at the original sequence names another key. That is a definite noncommit. Otherwise keep the fence. A queued row of the original key keeps the slot and goes to the exact inspect, expire or fence through Native.
- **RC9 with no result, or RC10 after the write starts.** Contact is possible. Wait for the exact result or a physical fence. No cold reader sends bytes or builds the command again.
- **RC11, RC12 or RC13 ACK lost.** Read back by the same key and fingerprint. Changed bytes or a changed receiver refuse.
- **Native exits after the RC8 commit (H4-3).** Native commits, loses its reply and exits, while Main keeps its waiter and the Harness exclusion. Main reads back the original key, or holds until owner recovery. The queue keeps exactly one row. The channel failure alone never releases the claim.

**Cold projection and exits.** On reopen, discard the live delivery authority, guards, waiters, monotonic instants, the Harness slot, the Main maps and every live claimant. A preserved raw source before RC4 is not an accepted Workspace receipt. The admitted effect path runs the cold `decide` before the capacity early return, after the existing guard for a held restore.

- **I-CL (S3b-r2, the class invariant).** No Native effect comes from a Main snapshot. A restore frame has an acquired authority only: the claim revision and generation that the owner itself wrote at RC3, and for the close arm also the close key that the owner itself committed at RC8. It carries the Sandbox, the Harness, the binding and the target that the owner's selection authorized, never a fresh read. A result replay validates and acknowledges the exact consumed result and posts no restore frame, so the latch stays. No site rebuilds a frame from a later read; a redelivery reuses the original frame or posts nothing. A lifecycle post that needs a guard refuses when the guard is missing: a guarded queue, a whole-Sandbox claim or rollback with no admitted owner, and a whole-Sandbox restore with no authority. The whole-Sandbox rollback is single use on the Main side. It is bound to the live S8 acquisition, consumed before its only post and discarded on owner loss or supersession. Its posting turn compares every child of the Sandbox with the pins of the claim's posting turn, and admits only the contiguous close keys that this eviction acknowledged. A lost reply is never posted again: the caller fences, and a committed rollback may already have restored admission. The private Main and Worker envelope is version 99, so a version-98 peer is refused before any mutation in both directions.
- **Cold-deferred (S3b-r2, Variant B, v0.2.0).** The cold path posts no restore frame. Where every cold rule attributes a latch to a claim of this lifecycle, the cold `decide` answers the fence `cold-deferred`. So a latch that a NanoCore stop strands between its capture receipt and its close queue (R2) keeps its fence, as do the other stop points (before the receipt, after the queue, during the dispatch, and between the close settlement and the live release). NanoHost keeps its Epoch across a NanoCore restart, and a draining Harness refuses the same-Epoch capacity inspection, the compatible reuse and the forced retirement. So one such latch keeps every new NanoHost Turn of the deployment refused. **Operator remedy:** restart the NanoHost process and wait for its fresh readiness. The new physical Epoch enables the absence-based eviction of the Sandbox. The independent holds still apply: for example, a stop before an accepted receipt can still require a Workspace capture disposition after the restart. The eligible cold restore below, and a same-Epoch exit for a fenced latch, are deferred past v0.2.0. The quiescent exit text below is the deferred design, kept as its record.
- **Quiescent Harness exit (P2, P3; deferred past v0.2.0).** The Native claim records no claimant, so a cold reader cannot name the latch owner. The cold `decide` compares full durable values, including the Harness revision. It restores a draining Harness only if every candidate history with the same durable values allows the same restore. All of these must be true at the post:
  - no new or outstanding producer: no live owner in this process holds the Harness slot or posts a claim, a close or a queue on the Harness;
  - every ambiguous writer is joined;
  - no queue cell of a binding on the Harness is queued, dispatched, unknown or unsettled;
  - the reader has the complete set of original capture anchors and dispositions on the Harness, under the owner order, and each is settled and none is abandoned. An unreadable or missing anchor keeps the fence;
  - a present binding on the Harness has an accepted capture that no close consumed. This is supporting evidence only. A receipt can survive an earlier restore, a later Harness-only drain and cell reuse;
  - the last operation of the cell proves that the drain began after it: the cell is idle with no operation, or its last operation is `session.open` or `turn.start`. The Native queue refuses both on a draining Harness, and a `harness.drain` result is the last operation until a later queue replaces it. Any other last operation can have replaced the marker of a Harness-only drain. The exit then fails closed and keeps the fence;
  - the positive predicates of the existing scoped restore hold: the Sandbox is accepting, open, ready and clean on the current Epoch; the Harness is open, not failed, and draining; the operation is idle or settled with no `harness.drain`;
  - the restore CAS reads the same Harness revision;
  - G3: the reader pins the claim generation of the row before it joins the capture proofs above, and it reuses that pinned value in the restore frame at the commit and at every redelivery. It never reads the generation again after the proofs. A null generation matches nothing and keeps the fence.

  Otherwise keep the fence. A failed Harness with an idle or settled cell on a clean accepting Sandbox keeps its drain. A reachable prefix with no exit keeps its fence for an engineer ruling.

  The cold `decide` fails closed: every restore that the durable values cannot attribute keeps its fence. The model check found these cases. Each keeps its fence until an engineer ruling:
  - **An open receipt that is out of date.** After an earlier restore, the receipt stays open until the next close of its binding. A Harness-only drain, then a later operation, leave every other condition above true. The last-operation condition keeps the fence. The cost: a stop between RC3 and RC8 keeps the Harness fenced when the last operation is not `session.open` or `turn.start`.
  - **An independent drain on a local claim.** A Harness-only drain lands on the drain of a local claim. Neither P7 nor the quiescent exit restores it.
  - **A sibling abandon.** A binding that never held the latch has an abandoned P1 capture on a Harness that another close drains. The restore is safe for the real history. Older double claims give the same durable values: a c1 claim after a settled close, stopped before its source and then abandoned, leaves the P7 proof of the settled close unchanged. So P7 and the quiescent exit both keep the fence. The live owner still releases its own claim once.
  - **A reused cell.** A close settled. Before its restore, a later operation reused the cell. Neither the P7 settle revision nor an open capture proves the claim, so the latch stays.
  - **A settled close with no live owner.** After A's Workspace claim, an independent `harness.drain` settles. The older Native claim then succeeds on the draining row, and A settles its close. The process stops before the release. All anchors are readable, none is abandoned, and the settle revision matches. An ordinary cell reuse in place of the drain gives the same durable values. So the cold P7 keeps the fence for both. The cost: a stop between RC12 and the live release keeps the Harness fenced.
- **Healthy-Epoch no-source abandon.** RC3 commits, then the process stops before the capture source or publication, then Workspace makes the governed no-source abandon. The Sandbox is accepting, the Harness is open and draining, and no cleanup intent exists. Capacity refuses it, the same-Epoch retirement refuses it, and the original cleanup reservation needs a draining Sandbox. An independently authorized cleanup that is already under way, or proved absence in a later fenced Epoch, exits only its own case. This prefix keeps its fence and its evidence and goes to an engineer ruling. An abandon alone grants no local restore and no wider delete.
- **E7.** The owner that makes the last local change requests the root reread after that change. A no-op restore or an intervening drain requests nothing. If another independent owner still blocks, that owner requests the reread at its own edge.

### Physical Epoch Identity And Recovery

`physicalEpoch` is a private non-secret physical-incarnation witness scoped to the configured NanoHost identity and deployment, derived once by NanoHost as the lowercase SHA-256 over the original UTF-8 bytes of the fresh `EpochPlan` epoch name (`apps/nanohost/src/epoch_coordinator.rs`, a named raw-byte digest exception to the RFC 8785 canonical JSON digest rule in [Protocol Identifiers And Enums](20261021-protocol_identifiers_and_enums.md)) that owns the private run/data roots and backend namespaces. The live coordinator retains and re-declares the same value on every healthy reconnect (including after a lost readiness acknowledgement), whereas a replacement coordinator derives a new value without reloading a predecessor witness or treating self-declaration alone as absence proof. The `RuntimeTarget` row owns nullable current `physicalEpoch` with its readiness projection; `SandboxRuntimeRecord` and pre-effect Worker backend-session rows own immutable non-null `originPhysicalEpoch` (inherited by Harness and AgentSession bindings without duplicate fields on attempts or storage associations). Normal origin writers read the current authenticated ready `RuntimeTarget` witness in the creating transaction and refuse absent, changed, conflicting, or non-ready authority; Sandbox reuse inherits the proved matching origin, and effect dispatch and Sandbox publication recheck the originating witness against current authenticated readiness (refusing publication on mismatch and preserving the original attempt/outcome for its cleanup owner). All cached and durable Sandbox, Harness, and AgentSession reuse requires matching current and originating witnesses alongside compatibility keys, lifecycle, scope, and authorization.

When pre-witness internal database records are carried forward, a bounded cold one-way storage conversion updates the three existing record schemas and migration ledger in one atomic transaction only after the operator verifies the complete predecessor NanoHost effect-domain fence, Core is stopped, and a complete external backup separate from the data root is verified. Converted origin rows receive the reserved `pre-witness` marker (never emitted by normal writers or treated as a live reusable origin; missing origin after conversion fails closed as corruption), `RuntimeTarget` witnesses become `null`/non-ready, all Tasks, Items, Artifacts, credentials, storage identities, attachment generations, and cleanup states are preserved, and repeat conversion is refused (failed preflight or transaction rolls back without mutation). Before `v0.1.0`, an explicit disclosed deployment reset under [Release Management](20260829-release_management.md#pre-stable-deployment-reset-and-protected-accounts) and [Pre-Stable Data Continuity](../decisions/20261014-pre_stable_data_continuity.md) may replace conversion only after proving the predecessor effect domain fenced while preserving protected accounts (conversion failure never authorizes an automatic reset or unknown-effect replay).

NanoHost decommission fences the authoritative connection, revokes all Tokens for the configured identity, stops admission, finishes or terminates work under operator lifecycle, invalidates and removes the complete supervised group, and removes raw slot files, Image Store content/index, and forensic reports/notes (while Worker volumes require separate purge authority and Core retains redacted history); later re-enrollment of the exact retained identity/deployment pair issues a fresh first Token and requires all ordinary readiness gates.

### Live Operator Inspection

The sole public App API readiness observation is server-admin `POST /api/app/operations/nanohost.runtime-target` (`{}`), returning the strict `NanoHostRuntimeTargetStatusResponseSchema` (`packages/app-api-schemas/src/nanohost.ts`; `404` when unconfigured) with authenticated `identityId`/`deploymentId`/`connectionGeneration` and ready/fenced/fresh-empty booleans joined to nullable stored `physicalEpoch` correlation, exposing no token, slot, capacity, or private host state and granting no runtime, placement, scheduling, or credential authority. Independently, `nanohost inspect` ([decision](../decisions/20261015-nanohost_live_operator_inspection.md), trusting the selected operator channel and host OS, with compromised host root outside its guarantee) provides a bounded read-only operator-channel probe before service initialization with stdin `{ "nonce": "<64 lowercase hexadecimal characters>" }` (`<= 256` UTF-8 bytes, response `<= 16 KiB`, CLI deadline `10 s`, server deadline `5 s`, and at most `4` concurrent blocking measurement jobs whose slots remain occupied until work ends without delaying supervision or fencing) over the coordinator-lifetime abstract Unix socket `openkit.nanohost.inspection.v1` with root peer-credential checks and pinned peer PID/incarnation. Observed success returns `nonce`, `outcome: "observed"`, `machineIdentityDigest` (lowercase raw-byte SHA-256 of the platform installation ID bytes under [Deployment Host Requirements](20260909-deployment_host_requirements.md) and [Protocol Identifiers And Enums](20261021-protocol_identifiers_and_enums.md)), `physicalEpoch`, `identityId`, `deploymentId`, `connectionGeneration` (recorded only after complete authenticated readiness acknowledgement and cleared on disconnect/fencing, with a private monotonic transition version detecting away-and-back reconnect races), `coordinator` and `executables.{nanohost,gateway}` (`bootId`, positive `pid`, `startTimeTicks`, and live `/proc` process-image raw-byte `sha256` hashed in `<= 64 KiB` chunks and rechecked before/after), `unit` (validated by `UNIT_PROPERTIES`, `UNIT_BINDING`, `unit_observation`, and `normalize_execution` in `apps/nanohost/src/live_inspection.rs`: `fileSha256` as raw-byte SHA-256 of the regular non-symlink unit file, loaded unit binding this measured coordinator as `MainPID` with `activeState: "active"`, `subState: "running"`, and `NeedDaemonReload=no`, and `effectiveStateDigest` as the named exception in `live_inspection.rs` computing lowercase SHA-256 over the sorted compact UTF-8 JSON string map with no trailing newline of the 40 required systemd properties—including empty values, refusing missing or duplicate properties, normalizing `ExecStart`/`ExecStop`/`ExecStopPost` into compact JSON arrays of sorted `{argv, ignoreErrors, path}` objects for the fixed accepted service command forms only while excluding volatile timestamps/PID/exit/counters and refusing unknown execution structures or value-bearing `UnsetEnvironment`, and omitting environment and secret values), and `replacementDuringMeasurement: false`. Any failure returns `{ "nonce": "<request nonce>", "outcome": "unavailable", "reason": "<not-running|access-denied|connection-unavailable|process-changed|measurement-unavailable|unit-unavailable>" }` with no partial envelope, repair, or retry; the [Installed Release Replacement](#installed-release-replacement) consumer joins both surrounding `nanohost.runtime-target` reads and live inspection on exact ready identity, deployment, generation, and `physicalEpoch`.

## Failure Scope And Recovery

### Core-Outage Restart Backoff

NanoHost uses one stock service unit with defaults `RestartSec=5s`, `RestartSteps=5`, and `RestartMaxDelaySec=5min`. On systemd 254 or later, stock systemd grows the restart delay geometrically up to the 5-minute cap; older supported managers ignore those two directives and retain the existing 5-second restart behavior as a documented degradation, with no version probing, alternate unit selection, or fixed 5-minute fallback. `RestartPreventExitStatus=78` preserves non-restartable exit when credentials are unavailable or admission returns HTTP `401`, keeping service-cgroup stop ordering, whole-Epoch fencing, and the `30 s` stop bound. A delayed restart proves neither cleanup nor a Turn outcome, adds no custom retry controller, timer file, or durable backoff counter, and continues automatic recoverable failures at the cap. Outside an explicit service-manager stop or prevented credential exit, the service manager terminates the complete failure group after NanoHost exit or kill and starts a fresh generation only after the complete previous group is observed gone ([decision](../decisions/20261002-nanohost_automatic_restart.md)); first start after installation remains an explicit operator action.

- **Local effect truth, Epoch fail-stop group, and `EpochCoordinator` monitor**: NanoHost holds truth only over its own definite accepted local effects (accepted by NanoCore without extending into product terminal meaning, and retaining only bounded private current-Epoch and result evidence with no durable journal resuming in-flight OpenShell operations after NanoHost death). The NanoHost service, stock Gateway, every effect-capable member of the configured container backend (`dockerd`, `containerd`, and their helpers and proxies), the `slirp4netns` helper, mutable runtime roots and networks, and all hosted Sandboxes belong to one OS-supervised fail-stop group; whole-epoch cgroup termination fences only this local process tree and never fences an external orchestrator or authorizes external backend effects. One `EpochCoordinator` and its monitor worker own the child handles and fence seam, polling all directly owned effect-capable children throughout short and long local effects, retaining one private unshared/uncloned OpenShell client, never restarting a member inside an Epoch, and killing and reaping every child once on normal drop or terminal member exit. Any uncertain accepted Sandbox create or delete, abnormal exit or identity change of an effect-capable member, or independent member restart invalidates the complete Runtime Epoch: NanoHost stops new admission, marks the slot unavailable, exports a bounded forensic report when alive, and exits so the supervisor terminates the complete group before fresh-empty recovery (with no member-local restart, PID/empty-list/lock/delete/delay substitute, or resource-local guessed success). Once the local runtime accepts a Sandbox create, NanoHost waits for definite success or failure even if NanoCore disconnects rather than timing out or cancelling local OpenShell execution; a definite failure that proves no Sandbox or pending effect remains preserves the healthy Epoch, whereas an uncertain outcome invalidates the Epoch.
- **Fresh-empty recovery, `physicalEpoch` witness fencing, and carrier cleanup**: fresh-empty readiness proves absence of prior processes, containers, backend/Gateway state, and authority rather than absence of retained user volume bytes; a fresh epoch cannot reattach a `storageRef` until the prior attachment's complete effect domain is fenced and Core freshly admits the same scope and compatible image layout, while an unknown create, detach, or replacement result blocks another writer or empty reinitialization and retains `unknown` or `cleanup_required`. Boot fencing, connection allocation, close, revocation, and readiness loss clear only current `RuntimeTarget` readiness and `physicalEpoch` without erasing immutable `originPhysicalEpoch` stamps; only a different freshly fenced and authenticated replacement coordinator permits truthful retirement and storage compare-and-swap of old physical handles, whereas same-Epoch reconnect, repeated readiness, advancing connection generation, or elapsed timestamps never prove writer absence or clear an uncertainty fence. A `credentialBindings` carrier receipt settles only after actual mounted source, destination, access, and helper proof; unknown import or mount blocks work and invokes cleanup, exact Sandbox deletion releases the carrier only after process absence, complete prior-Epoch fencing permits strict unmount or removal of a generated root-owned empty directory, and foreign contents, symlinks, or uncertain mounts refuse cleanup and fence the Epoch. Exceeding the `300 s` hard rebuild bound is measured and reported as a violation and keeps that process non-ready without weakening readiness proofs. The fence-to-ready timestamp is create-once within an unresolved interval, preserved across faults and evidence export, and bounds request construction, send, and complete empty `204` readiness acknowledgement. At the next process start, after the supervisor proves the complete predecessor effect domain absent, NanoHost exports the existing absent-epoch disposition, moves the prior epoch roots and first-fence marker into private bounded recovery custody, records a new interval and starts a fresh empty Epoch. Custody retains only the newest completed set, with an interrupted replacement retaining its predecessor until the moves complete; each root stays on its own filesystem. No retained Worker bytes, Image Store content, credential slots or evidence format changes, and forensic contents never authorize recovery.
- **NanoCore restart, execution-server restart, and compatibility/image failure isolation**: NanoCore restart, upgrade, or short network outage does not invalidate a healthy Runtime Epoch or recreate a Sandbox; the worker's process, attempt, sequence, and local runtime state survive within the bounded control-plane outage budget while inference availability during the outage is zero (NanoHost never opens a direct provider path, caches responses or received authority, broadens deadlines, or infers completion), and reconnect establishes a strictly increasing connection generation on the same exact attempt, AgentSession, backend session, package lineage, process key, and worker sequence without duplicate creation, launch, output, or capacity claims (otherwise preserving the owner's truthful `interrupted`, `unknown`, or `cleanup_required` result, while closing or cancelling the bootstrap monitor, bridge, or HTTP/2 connection is never a kill or process-absence proof). Execution-server restart invalidates every Runtime Epoch on that host (`interrupted` or `unknown` without inferred cancellation, completion, or cleanup) and keeps NanoHost non-ready until the prior effect domain is proved impossible and fresh roots, networks, namespaces, Gateway, and backend are healthy and empty; permanent host loss has no automatic migration and requires an explicit newly configured NanoHost deployment. An equal static Harness descriptor reuses the Harness within capacity while a different descriptor selects another Harness; a static update drains the predecessor and deletes it after bindings close, restart adopts only an exact surviving descriptor and key, missing/stale/duplicate/unsupported/over-capacity/dependency-failed evidence blocks placement and drains/fences the narrowest provable boundary, failed first creation leaves no reusable Harness, and uncertain creation widens cleanup. An empty Image Store is valid at readiness; a missing local digest has no registry or build fallback; a corrupt Image Store entry is discarded on re-verification without repair; and a missing/corrupt entry or definite registry retrieval, authorized build, or point-of-use import failure fails only the dependent attempt or preparation candidate without invalidating a healthy Epoch (build results are consumed only by digest with no tag/push/published image, and optional build cache is inert digest-keyed content discarded on verification failure).
- **Proved-clean Sandbox create failure**: a Sandbox in phase Error, a ready timeout, or a definite Gateway rejection before an accepted Sandbox id fails only its `sandbox.create` request after exact expected-name deletion proves absence, mounted credentials are removed, and the retained-volume attachment is released. The admitted Gateway rejections are `InvalidArgument`, `FailedPrecondition` and `ResourceExhausted` before an accepted id. Every typed readiness observation must retain the accepted id and name, including Error and Pending; identity mismatch remains terminal. The fixed ready deadline and the complete delete-plus-absence proof each remain bounded at `120 s`. A proved-clean seed failure, including copy timeout, helper spawn failure or extractor panic, follows the same request boundary. The result uses the existing `effect_failed` outcome and retained-result delivery; it grants no automatic create replay. An uncertain create, deletion, credential cleanup or attachment release still invalidates the Epoch. Before first readiness, a refused Core TCP connection retries only within the current process startup deadline; trust, identity and readiness failures keep their existing guards.

- **Summary of failure boundaries across local and session surfaces**: local preparation-image persistence failure returns empty `503` and retries identical result delivery every `1 s` on the same connection without interrupting resident Workers or replaying commands ([NanoHost Transport](20261116-nanohost_transport.md)); an authoritative successor that starts its first effect poll with no eligible retained mutating result marks the prior effect `unknown` once, fences capacity, and fail-stops the Epoch; exact local `session.close` preserves compatible siblings while uncertain close widens to the Harness, Sandbox, or Epoch boundary; failed off-peak freshness rebuild leaves the old or partial Sandbox non-admitting with zero advertised capacity; an unconfigured or external-domain container backend fails before readiness; NanoHost Token slot installation, rotation, abort, or clear does not disturb a live Epoch (re-reading slots only on the next connection attempt); and reaching the produced-fact buffer size (`8 MiB`) or age bound fails affected records truthfully without dropping, summarizing, or reordering.

### Epoch Invalidation Report

The Epoch Invalidation Report is NanoHost-private, non-product, non-authorizing forensic evidence exported to an append-only host location owned by the NanoHost service account before fail-stop termination; it is never a product `EvidenceBundle` ([Audit, Usage, And Evidence Records](20260703-audit_usage_evidence_records.md)), Core protocol field, durable operation journal, recovery/adoption/settlement input, or scheduling/readiness input (Audit receives only a redacted reference).
- **NanoHost-initiated versus NanoHost-absent triggers**: when NanoHost is alive and invalidates an epoch, it uses one of the nine closed triggers — `uncertain-create`, `uncertain-delete`, `member-exit`, `member-identity-change`, `member-local-restart`, `containment-loss`, `epoch-creation-failure`, `operator-action`, or `terminal-session-failure` (used when closed outer-session failure rules require terminal exit of a ready Epoch and no more specific trigger applies, preserving the first unresolved fence timestamp) — and exports one report before group termination and process exit. When NanoHost is already absent (`nanohost-crash`, `nanohost-killed`, `nanohost-identity-loss`, `execution-server-restart`, or `host-loss`), no report is synthesized; instead, the next epoch's recovery records one bounded **prior-epoch disposition note** (`nanohost-absent`, prior epoch identity/generation if recoverable, observed residual processes/roots/networks/sockets/sandboxes, and fencing outcome) in the same location without delaying or weakening the fence.
- **Redacted contents and bounds**: a report contains only epoch identity/generation, creation and invalidation timestamps, configured NanoHost identity id, trigger, uncertain operation kind/lineage/loss point/elapsed time, effect-capable member inventory with PIDs/resolved binary identities/cgroup/observed exit code, signal, or task-finished reason (or unavailable proof, never unverified exit codes or raw exception text), configured backend identity/version and imported image digests, readiness proof results, current connection generation/fence/bridge state, and a bounded redacted tail of member diagnostics. It MUST NOT contain raw or hashed NanoHost Tokens, sink contents, Gateway/backend/provider/Vault credentials, TLS private material, prompts, transcripts, or Workspace/Artifact bytes. Each report or note is bounded to at most `8 MiB` and `2 s` of export time (truncated and marked incomplete if reached, never delaying the fence), and at most the `20` most recent reports or notes are retained before pruning the oldest.

## Acceptance

### Replacement Close Lifecycle Conformance

- **Model and explorer before executor changes.** Verify the [Replacement Close Lifecycle](#replacement-close-lifecycle) with the pure `decide` table and an independent environment model. The environment generates only the writes that a real owner can make. It records posts, commits, ACKs, committed captures, preserved raw sources, accepted receipts and occupancy, and handed-off bytes as separate facts. The oracle checks the original owner, the contact count and the available capacity from those raw facts, not from the table under test.
- **Bounds.** Two bindings A and B on one Harness. One binding on another Harness of the same Sandbox as a control. Two competing polls. Two target generations. One whole-Sandbox drain, one Harness-only drain, one Native queued expiry and one dispatched unknown. Tc, Ts and Ta advance independently, and Ta folds a second required credential. The wall steps back before the enqueue instant at any point. A clock can also pass, or the wall step back, after any fact read of RC7, RC8 or RC10, before the effect. In one bounded world, a second move follows in the same RC7 or RC10 gather: the wall steps back below the deadline that a clock-checking read of that gather saw pass, above the enqueue instant (H4-1). In a second one, the monotonic Tc fails first, the wall passes Tc and Ta, and the step back goes below both. An independent drain can settle at the claim's own stamp. A rotation shortens the issuer grace after the ACK (H4-2). Native can exit after its commit while Main lives (H4-3). The reuse writers move a released row to a lower stamp, and a committed restore frame can arrive again (G3). At each reachable commit, the explorer also generates a lost ACK, a stopped producer and a reopen with no peer, and runs the same `decide` on the durable projection. Report the state and edge counts, the shortest failing trace for each mutation and every runnable state with no successor. Liveness assumes that owners recover and that legal operations are scheduled. A cold step must be safe for every history with the same full durable values, including the Harness revision and the claim generation, with every live claimant discarded.
- **Mutations and controls.** The unmodified model passes. Count red mutations and green controls separately. Each mutation fails at its named assertion: the actual-post clocks and identity check, the deciding queue cutoff, the RC10 rechecks, the claim-to-queue transfer, each independently recoverable cold exit, each positive restore predicate, the generation compare in each reuse schedule and at RC8, the cleared generation on the written restore row, the carried R and P14 clock causes, the failed monotonic Tc in the cause minimum, and the last E7 request. Where G3 alone stops an older single take-out, its mutation takes out G3 too, and a separate check shows that G3 alone stops the single take-out. Green controls stay green: CL3's two winner orders, the legacy double-claim traces, B's fresh claim after A's proved release, two Harnesses, a whole-Sandbox drain, a failed Harness and a redundant earlier notification. Each exit that makes the Harness accepting must admit one fresh close. The F3 report counts the durable projection at each crash edge apart from the activity after the restart. After wiring, each mutation fails again at a production assertion. A setup failure, an escaping injected exception or a timeout earns no credit.

### Epoch Rebuild Cost Budget

| Budget | V1 target | Hard bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fence proved to readiness advertised, total, including an empty Image Store | at most `90 s` | at most `300 s`; report a violation, then permit a fresh bounded rebuild at the next proved process start |
| Fresh roots, backend start, Gateway health, and all remaining readiness proofs, inside the total above | at most `30 s` | at most `120 s` |
| Point-of-use cold image load from the store, outside readiness | at most `15 s` | one absolute `300 s` bound, capped by any earlier caller deadline, then fail the dependent operation |
| Bounded image metadata commands, including post-load manifest inspection, outside readiness | outside readiness | at most `45 s` per command, capped by any earlier caller deadline |
| Registry retrieval into the store, off the readiness path | not budgeted for readiness | at most `15 min` per image, then fail the attempt |
| Authorized build into the store, off the readiness path | not budgeted for readiness | at most `30 min`, at most `20 GiB` OCI output, and at most `128` manifest layers, then fail the build |

The shared-epoch premise is falsified if, measured over at least `200` completed AgentSessions on the declared topology, either the uncertain-outcome rate exceeds `1` per `100` completed AgentSessions or the observed rebuild time exceeds the `300 s` hard bound; sustained violation reopens the epoch-granularity decision rather than returning silently to per-AgentSession runtimes or grace-based cleanup.

- **Service backoff, readiness gates, and `physicalEpoch` witness**: verify the single bundled systemd unit, modern stepped restart backoff (`RestartSec=5s`, `RestartSteps=5`, `RestartMaxDelaySec=5min`), older-manager 5-second degradation (tolerating only exact stock ignored-key warnings for those two directives in `[Service]`), prevented exit `78`, and stock systemd counter resets (requiring restored Core connectivity and an authenticated ready Host for deliberate operator reset). Verify all readiness preconditions (current identity and authoritative session, active transport Token, exact `0.0.99` Rust/SDK/Gateway/Supervisor identities, foreground cgroup membership, fresh private network/mount namespaces and DNS, single declared host-local backend, healthy loopback Gateway, empty container/Sandbox inventory, private external Image Store with no Worker image preload, prior-epoch fence, and `EpochCoordinator::start` fresh-slot proof) and the authenticated `nanohost.runtime-target` readiness observation (`identityId` and `deploymentId` match, `predecessorFenced`, `ready`, and `freshEmpty` all `true`, and post-start current generation). Verify `physicalEpoch` witness continuity across same-Epoch reconnect versus different-Epoch retirement and storage reattachment, pre-effect backend anchor cleanup without a Sandbox, witness cutover backup/rollback/repeat refusal, and original-volume readback.
- **Shared-Sandbox topology, compatibility keys, and persistent volumes**: verify on exact-build real-host runs that persistent-volume attachments and replacements preserve user bytes across normal deletion and epoch recovery, identical Sandbox inputs share one Sandbox, different Harness descriptors select at least two distinct Harnesses inside one Sandbox, each Harness opens at least two AgentSessions for distinct Threads (e.g., Codex AgentSessions with distinct private `CODEX_HOME` roots, native handles, children, Turn slots, credentials, and cleanup proofs, pending first-Turn handle establishment, resident native process reuse across sequential Turns, exact native conversation resume on a successor, fresh per-Turn AEP/Context/route tokens, and exact local `session.close` preserving siblings), equal Harness descriptors reuse within capacity, incompatible AgentSession inputs reject continuity reuse, restart adopts only exact surviving keys without approximate fallback, and diagnostics claim only conversation-context and Workspace-write isolation rather than compromise-security isolation.
- **Image Store, retained-parent build, distribution, replacement, and live inspection**: verify no-readiness Worker image preload, bounded-memory streaming archive verification/admission and rewound-descriptor backend import (exercising a layer larger than buffering limits so memory stays independent of archive size), retained-parent Buildx builds under strict policy in a fresh backend with registries unavailable (covering alias reuse, conflicting alias, conflicting archive annotation, wrong resolved checksum, broader-rule bypass, duplicate references, bounded import timeout, Epoch recreation, and manifest-versus-configuration inspection), and strict `build-context://empty/v1` + inline Dockerfile (`1..268,435,456` bytes) + `layerLimit` (`1..128`) + `{docker.io:443, ghcr.io:443}` / AEP `{host, port}` egress enforcement before any `RUN` instruction. Verify byte-reproducible `linux/amd64` and `linux/arm64` archives, `nanohost --version` before effects, verifier rejection of corruption/mismatch/conflict/staging escape, isolated fixed-path `--check-host` and all four `--check`/install dispositions, stopped Installed Release Replacement across payload subsets with complete cgroup fencing, persistent inhibition, exact byte recovery, and forward/reset compatibility, and `nanohost inspect` positive and negative cases (unauthorized peer, malformed request, wrong nonce, inaccessible files, live-image versus pathname replacement, process exit/replacement, unit change, and same-Epoch reconnect race).
- **Required fault scenarios and blocking preconditions**:
  1. **NanoCore restart during active work**: start a real AgentSession, observe a post-launch durable worker sequence, restart only NanoCore while NanoHost, the Runtime Epoch, Sandbox, Integration, and worker continue, establish a fenced successor transport session, and complete through one accepted `final_status` with exact attempt/AgentSession/backend-session/package/process-key/sequence continuity and zero duplicate Sandbox creates, launches, output publications, inference/capability replays, or capacity claims.
  2. **NanoHost `SIGKILL` during blocked Sandbox create**: hold an accepted `sandbox.create` below the NanoHost operation boundary (observed via the value-free pre-dispatch journal record carrying only the closed operation name), deliver `SIGKILL` to NanoHost without manual cleanup, prove the OS supervisor terminates the Gateway, backend members, Sandboxes, cgroups, and helpers with no late effect after fault release, and recover through a fresh-empty Epoch before advertising capacity.
  3. **Execution-server restart**: restart the execution server during a live or in-flight operation, prove NanoCore marks affected AgentSessions `interrupted` or `unknown` without inferring cancellation or completion, keep NanoHost non-ready while any old effect or state remains possible, and prove the fresh Gateway and backend healthy and empty before accepting work.
  4. **Effect-capable member failure**: kill the stock Gateway or a configured backend member during an accepted operation without first killing NanoHost, prove the coordinator monitor invalidates and terminates the entire supervised group without member-local restart, interrupt hosted AgentSessions independently with zero usable capacity, and recover only through a fresh empty Epoch that blocks old member identities from re-entering.
  5. **Cutover and blocking preconditions**: NanoHost is the sole selectable runtime path without legacy Cell, SSH lifecycle, Gateway-forward, or direct-endpoint fallback; stock integration qualification ([NanoHost OpenShell Gateway](20261116-nanohost_openshell_gateway.md)), saturating-load transport envelope ([NanoHost Transport](20261116-nanohost_transport.md)), and the rebuild-budget premise are measured blocking preconditions with no alternate authority or open design waivers, and local source/regression evidence does not substitute for fresh exact-candidate live-host qualification.
