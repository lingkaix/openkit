# Change Execution Governance

## Purpose And Scope

This document owns coordination for material repository work: preserving user intent, selecting and revising a method, composing agent capabilities, retaining enough evidence to resume, and deciding when to continue, reframe, ask the engineer, or close. It also owns the minimum content and lifecycle of material change records.

It does not own product design, architecture, test layers, evidence quality, documentation types, toolchain setup, or role-specific operation. Those remain with accepted Core and specifications, `docs/specs/20260529-test_strategy.md`, `docs/verification-instruments.md`, `docs/documentation-model.md`, `docs/toolchain.md`, and `docs/roles/` respectively. A change record tracks work and never authorizes design.

The primary judges materiality and assurance provisionally from consequence, uncertainty, reversibility, and coordination need. Root `AGENTS.md` names signals, not an exhaustive classifier or automatic risk tier. All material work follows this document; a durable change record is needed only when context must survive or the engineer requires a plan. Ordinary scoped work executes directly. Revise assurance when evidence changes the concern. More files, agents, or uncertainty alone does not mandate every role.

## Intent And Working Record

Preserve the source of the requested outcome, non-negotiables, acceptance observations, exclusions, effect boundary, and accepted decisions, alongside current facts, material unknowns, and owner links. Keep consequential engineer wording close to its source; distinguish an agent's inference from an engineer decision. Record each engineer ruling with its reason and revisit trigger when it is made, in the change record while the work runs and as a decision record under `docs/documentation-model.md` when its rule lands in an owner. A missing source is missing evidence, not permission to reconstruct agreement. Do not predict a complete future queue, fixed role order, correction count, artifact inventory, or implementation magnitude.

For material long-running work, preserve the user's Intent as append-only Intent Revision entries: a recorded Intent Revision MUST NOT be modified, rewritten, or deleted. A change to the outcome, non-negotiables, acceptance, or effect boundary appends a new revision with its source and reason. Plans written before 2026-09-24 call these entries Intent Epochs, and their headings stay as written. Current facts, unknowns, method, frontier, and Next Action live in a rewritable checkpoint. The checkpoint is a recovery aid, not authority or proof, and its consequential claims are reconciled with Git, the owned artifact, named evidence, or the relevant external system on resume.

A plan is a working prediction. Evidence may revise its method, decomposition, role composition, test order, or frontier without engineer approval when user intent, authority, strict safety, and accepted product decisions remain unchanged. Historical queues, matrices, assignments, gates, and state files remain evidence of what was once predicted or done; they do not control later dispatch.

## Adaptive Execution

The primary agent coordinates material work and keeps recorded Intent, accepted authority, and the Safety Kernel invariant while methods change. It reconciles reports with actual artifacts, chooses and integrates the next useful probe or action, assigns one writer per repository path, and delegates only when another context adds useful production capacity or independence. Its responsibility is convergence, not maximizing role calls or handoffs.

Registered roles are capabilities, not a fixed pipeline. Use a researcher for independent external evidence, a test author to derive expected behavior independently, and a builder when the owner, outcome, and writable boundary are clear. A reviewer normally inspects material artifacts for correctness, completeness, owner alignment, and simplicity before acceptance. A Consultant challenges value, premises, feasibility, simpler alternatives, and continued investment; it states which gap it fills, freshness, independence, or capability, and takes the matching stance of challenging or guiding, so no separate advisor role exists. An Auditor examines fidelity across source intent, recorded decisions, accepted authority, projections, implementation, and observed behavior. A writer drafts or edits documentation text and never changes a criterion; another context checks every rewrite against the diff. Select the question and useful independence, not a fixed order or invocation count; Consultant and Reviewer are not automatically paired.

Roles have a position and a function. Position is relative to one scope of work: a dispatcher hands the scope over, the primary owns its intent, integration, and acceptance, and delegates do parts of it. Each scope has exactly one primary, and positions nest: an agent that receives a scope is the primary of that scope and dispatches its own delegates, and the product Orchestrator is a dispatcher in this sense. Function is the question a context answers: researcher, test author, builder, writer, reviewer, Consultant, or Auditor, each with a contract under `docs/roles/`. The primary may perform any function itself, which is the better choice when the context a delegate would need cannot be handed over without loss; the independence rules below still apply to what the primary produces. The decision and its reason are recorded in [a decision record](decisions/20260924-role_positions_and_functions.md).

Each role contract names a minimum capability tier: frontier, the strongest available model, for work that carries judgment; standard, a strong general model; and fast, a cheap and quick model. Model family and context size are separate attributes, and `docs/agent-harnesses.md` binds tiers to models. The primary of material work, the Consultant, and the Auditor are frontier. A reviewer is at least the tier of the producer it reviews and, for consequential work, preferably of another model family. A fast delegate receives only bounded work whose result is cheaper to check than to produce, through a bounded task contract that states its inputs, writable paths, completion check, prohibitions, and return shape. The decision and its reason are recorded in [a decision record](decisions/20260924-prompt_dispatched_roles.md). These default compositions are starting points that evidence may change, not pipelines:

- Ordinary work: the primary and one independent [reviewer](roles/reviewer.md).
- Material work that can run in parallel: a frontier primary, delegates for bounded side work such as a fast [builder](roles/builder.md), and a reviewer.
- Heavy building: add a [test author](roles/test-author.md) separate from the builder, so that the producer does not also set its own pass condition, and name each seam in the plan.
- Direction-bearing, consequential, or long-running work: add a [Consultant](roles/consultant.md) before substantial investment, fresh direction checks at their triggers, and an [Auditor](roles/auditor.md).
- Degraded mode, when frontier capacity is short: a standard primary obtains a frontier context at objective triggers, namely a Safety Kernel concern, a seam between different owners, a failing check still unexplained after one Reframe, or an ambiguous specification, rather than when it judges itself out of its depth.

Delegate a side task when the main line needs its conclusion but not its detail, which preserves the primary's context budget. The delegate returns a conclusion with resolvable evidence pointers such as paths, commands, and citations, and the primary samples them by risk. Decompose along owner seams, because dispatch boundaries become code and document boundaries. A parallel dispatch names the writer of each path and the single owner of each interpretation rule the delegates share. When a change alters a representation that crosses different owners, such as packages or effect domains, including encoding and serialization, chunking, identity and order, or a durability handoff, the primary names the seam in the plan and assigns a test author who reads the owning contract of each side; the test strategy owns the test that proves the seam, and a reviewer checks that the seam was named.

An agent-initiated challenge to an accepted governing design names the owning decision, the evidence against it, the proposed amendment, the simpler alternative, and the behavior and risk affected. An independent Consultant scrutinizes the challenge before the engineer decides it. Agent agreement is not design approval. Preserve material objections and the engineer's ruling in the current change record, then update the accepted owner before dependent implementation. The primary distinguishes this governing-design case from ordinary implementation choices, documentation corrections, and refactors within accepted design; those do not acquire a fixed role pipeline or new approval gate. An engineer's explicit delegation of concrete implementation choices remains authorization within its stated boundary.

Before substantial commitment to an uncertain or consequential new direction, the primary writes a proposal proportional to the unresolved choices: source intent, proposed outcome and method, relevant alternatives including doing less, decisive assumptions, and the cheapest observations that could defeat it. An independent Consultant examines those sources and actual facts before adopting the primary's conclusion. Discussion and probes revise the proposal until both find a reasonable, feasible route without an unresolved material objection; agreement alone proves neither optimality nor feasibility. A proposal that introduces a new concept answers whether it can be an instance of an existing kind, whether it can register into existing dispatch instead of changing every operation, and whether its durable state can be derived from existing facts. An important interface change is shown first as a short call example with its errors and side effects, because the engineer decides module responsibilities and key seams while agents implement inside them. Record the reasons, evidence, and remaining assumptions, then create or update the execution plan and retain worthwhile rationale as the optional proposal below. Exploration stays under `temp/`; accepted design goes to its existing owner. Settled directions and ordinary corrections need no separate proposal or consultation ceremony.

This document authorizes incremental Auditor scrutiny during material work, including a disputed assurance choice or signs of intent drift, without a separate audit commission. Inspect the affected source-to-authority-to-artifact path and relevant unchanged consumers; the whole corpus is not the default scope. Within discretion left by existing authority, the Auditor may settle the named assurance disagreement, require relevant evidence, remove unsupported ceremony, or pause the affected commitment. Report the concern, supporting facts, ruling, and what would change it in the current task context. Retain it in a change record when one exists or the context independently needs to survive; an Auditor call alone requires neither a change record nor a dated audit. This cannot waive a MUST, the Safety Kernel, an accepted owner, or an engineer decision, create authority through a report, or resolve a real governing conflict without the engineer. Routine corrections use existing findings and test paths. A durable cross-task integrity audit is dated under `docs/audits/` and links this document, or a more specific owning rule; calibration and terminal archives retain their own owners and separation requirements.

The primary may steer or replace test-author, builder, reviewer, Consultant, or Auditor contexts when attention, misunderstanding, or method impedes progress. Supply source intent, the relevant owner, actual artifacts, evidence, and unresolved objections; allow the recipient to derive its own expectations. Never prescribe a passing verdict, hide dissent, or replace a disagreeing role merely to obtain agreement. Stop the previous writer before transferring the same paths. All roles may raise a direction concern and request independent scrutiny or human involvement; pause only work whose validity depends on resolving it. Self-correction is useful, but a co-designer or producer cannot independently accept the resulting authority-bearing artifact, even under a new role name.

Before a material, delegated, expensive, repeated, or effectful Next Action, record what Artifact, Belief, or Decision it is expected to change, the expected Observable, and the Evidence that would change or reframe the route. Simple reads, searches, and obvious local checks need no separate record. After the action, record the observation rather than rewriting the prediction. Repeating the same method without a new hypothesis, evidence, or expected change is not progress.

Signals that the route may have stalled include inability to name the next Observable; report, handoff, or status churn without a changed Artifact, Belief, or Decision; conflict between a report and the artifact; recurrence of the same defect class across adjacent surfaces; or coordination machinery growing faster than the outcome. These signals are not counters, breakers, or automatic role calls. Respond with the cheapest direct probe or local correction, then `Reframe` when evidence defeats the premise or method, or use a fresh-context direction check when the primary context may be drifting. Resolve role disagreement against the accepted owner and direct artifact evidence, never by vote.

Every material turn has exactly four possible outcomes: `Continue` when an Artifact, Belief, or Decision changed and a useful next step remains; `Reframe` when evidence defeated the premise, plan, proxy, or method; `Ask Human` when an intent, trade-off, authority, strict-effect, important residual-risk, unresolved consequential disagreement, or viable-path decision needs the engineer; or `Close` when acceptance is settled by direct artifact or observation. Activity, status updates, role handoffs, and gate narration are not additional outcomes.

Use `Continue` for bounded correction inside the accepted direction and `Reframe` before repeating a defeated method. Before `Ask Human`, run the cheapest useful authorized probe or independent direction intervention when available; do not invent endless probes when no credible route emerges. Actively invite the engineer with the unresolved choice, facts, and recommendation if supportable; uncertainty may prevent a recommendation. Missing authorization pauses its effect immediately. `Close` reports exact evidence and unresolved findings without converting either into authority.

In material long-running work a fresh context MUST check direction when the primary has compacted since its own last check, before a direction-bearing commitment: changing scope or accepted design, substantial investment in a new route, an irreversible or external effect, or closure whose delivered scheme materially differs from the scrutinized direction. An ordinary commit or cross-owner delegation within that direction is not sufficient by itself. After a Reframe, a long pause and resume, or primary-agent replacement, check before renewed material investment. Coverage is per plan and per primary-context identity: a delegated context's own compaction is not the primary's, and a check clears the obligation of the primary context whose Intent, checkpoint, and evidence it read, not of the fresh context that performed it. Ordinary mid-slice compaction is not a dispatch trigger; where the plan keeps a route log, record one line naming what was re-read and stating that no fresh check ran. That line is self-reported rather than proof.

A Consultant may name one concrete evidence or stage trigger for the next fresh direction observation in the checkpoint when the route could become locally convincing but globally unproductive. At that trigger the primary MUST obtain the independent observation before dependent work continues, even if it sees no drift itself. Do not invent a trigger without a meaningful observable. Add no recurring timer or numeric activity quota. The fresh context reads source Intent, checkpoint, actual Git or artifact state, and named evidence, tests whether the next investment still advances the outcome, and returns only `Continue`, `Reframe`, `Ask Human`, or `Close` with its reason. It does not repeat implementation review or certify global optimality. A context that may be drifting does not certify its own recovery. The primary must actually dispatch; instruction checks cannot prove freshness or guarantee an undispatched intervention.

## Evidence And Acceptance

Facts come from direct artifacts and observations. Before accepting work, inspect the actual diff, bytes, or named execution output; a producer report is never sufficient by itself. A producer may self-review and correct its work but may not independently adjudicate an authority-bearing artifact it produced. Independent judgment is selected where consequence, uncertainty, or producer bias makes it material, not because a tier mandates a role sequence.

When an instrument or oracle will decide a gate, classify and admit it under `docs/verification-instruments.md`; never retain a gate by substituting a proxy that cannot observe its subject. A failing check proves the named defect only when the expected deciding path ran. Builders do not weaken, delete, skip, or bypass contract-derived checks to obtain green.

Verification scope is recomputed from the actual final diff. Run the lowest-sufficient focused checks while iterating; run broader package, repository, release, real-environment, or strict-effect checks when the changed surface or accepted boundary requires them. Preserve secrets, authorization, cleanup, external effects, and data-loss controls regardless of ordinary proportionality.

Findings outside the current outcome do not silently expand it. Record a finding with its evidence and likely owner; continue current work when safe. It becomes work only through user intent or an accepted owner. A defect within the current outcome is corrected directly or causes a reframe.

## Change Records And Retention

Create or update one change record under `docs/changes/` when material context must survive the chat or commit diff. A plan records its Intent Revisions, current checkpoint, owner links, accepted decisions, material evidence, current status, and eventual closeout. It does not need a future package queue or machine-readable coordination controller.

New change plans use a bundle directory named `YYYYMMDDHHMMSSssss-short_name/` with required `plan.md` and optional `proposal.md`, `findings.md`, and `route-log.md`. A legacy `state.json` may remain unchanged as opaque historical evidence. Do not create, normalize, or append coordination events for the new process. No active authority may depend on a change record to interpret current behavior.

An optional `proposal.md` preserves why the work and scheme were chosen, alternatives considered, decisive assumptions and evidence, and material Consultant objections with their resolution or remaining uncertainty. It is a work-proposal rationale record with no independent design authority. Explore under `temp/` first; when entering implementation and creating the change plan, retain only worthwhile argument in the bundle. `plan.md` references it rather than copying the scheme. A few sentences in the plan or an existing Draft specification that fully explains the proposal are sufficient; do not create a duplicate file. During execution update it only for a material scheme change, stating the reason; Git preserves history without version numbers, lifecycle states, or a dedicated approval mechanism. After close it remains historical evidence, without an obligation to track the evolving product. Promote lasting design and its rationale to the owning Core or specification.

A `route-log.md` carries its plan's route history in one required shape and no other: one plain line per entry, appended by an agent at a decision point and never by machinery. The three approved pilot plans keep one; any other plan may, and none is required to. It carries four entry kinds and nothing else: a `Reframe` with the evidence that defeated the premise; a failed attempt or refuted hypothesis with the missing fact it named; a direction check, written by the fresh context that performed it, with its outcome and reason; and, for an ordinary mid-slice compaction, the one line naming what was re-read and stating that no fresh check ran. A commit already records the `Continue` outcome and the artifact, so neither is repeated here; a committing turn's defeated premise, refuted hypothesis, or direction verdict is still an entry. The primary agent owns the append, as it owns every other single-writer path. The file is non-authorizing execution evidence: `plan.md` keeps Intent, key design, accepted decisions, and the rewritable checkpoint, and gains no working log, while `findings.md` keeps this plan's findings and their dispositions. The entry kinds state what an entry carries, not a checked format: add no schema file, validator, test, revision counter, or automatic writer to it. Its completeness is self-reported and no check establishes it. At closeout the primary agent promotes its surviving conclusions to their owners.

A `findings.md` item has exactly three statuses and a new finding starts as `open`. `open` means the finding remains unresolved and the current plan still owes its correction, decision, or admission. `deferred` means the finding remains unresolved but an accepted owner or recorded engineer decision moved it outside the current plan and the last sentence of its `Next action` names the receiver and activation condition. `closed` means no work remains under that finding because an accepted correction, decision, supersession, or historical disposition settled it; `closed` is terminal, and recurrence creates a new item whose `Observation` cites the old findings path and ID. The permitted transitions are `open` → `closed`, `open` → `deferred`, `deferred` → `open` when accepted work readmits it, and `deferred` → `closed` when later work settles it. The item is the source when its index projection disagrees. A transition updates the heading and Follow-up Index in the same commit, appends its transition history to `Next action`, appends the closing fields when it reaches `closed`, and deletes no substantive history. The primary agent of the accepted change that performs a later transition takes exclusive write ownership of that earlier `findings.md` path for the transition.

Each finding heading is exactly `## [<status>] <ID> — <short title>`. For a new report, `<PLAN-PREFIX>` is one uppercase ASCII label matching `[A-Z][A-Z0-9]*`, selected once within that report, and every new ID is `<PLAN-PREFIX>-FND-<three digits>` unique within the file; an existing ID remains stable, including the historical `F-<number>` form, and no ID is reused, renumbered, or deleted. A citation outside the report names both its repository-relative `findings.md` path and the ID because IDs are not globally unique. Each item uses only fixed, non-empty, one-line Markdown bullets of the exact shape ``- **<Label>:** <text>`` and admits no other heading or body form. The fields appear in this order: `Observation`, `Impact`, `Evidence`, `Owner`, `Next action`, then `Closing verdict` and `Closure evidence` only when closed. Every item requires the first five fields. Open and deferred items forbid both closing fields; closed items require both `Closing verdict` and `Closure evidence`. `Observation` states the defect, `Impact` names affected owners and the consequence if it remains, `Evidence` names the observation and provenance without secret or untrusted raw content, `Owner` names the accepted receiver or says that none is accepted, and `Next action` is append-only transition history whose last sentence is current for an open or deferred item and whose complete text remains historical after closure. `Closing verdict` records the accepted reason no work remains, and `Closure evidence` names the exact artifact or observation that supports that verdict. A missing historical fact is written as `Not recorded.` rather than reconstructed.

After any permitted optional frontmatter, every findings report starts with `# Findings`, one non-authorizing preamble paragraph, and `## Follow-up Index` before its items, and admits no other headings except its item headings. The Follow-up Index is a non-authorizing projection and a marking surface, not the source of a finding: every `open` and `deferred` item appears exactly once in document order using the exact unchecked shape ``- [ ] `<ID>` [<status>] <short title>``; a previously listed item that later becomes `closed` remains in place using the exact checked shape ``- [x] `<ID>` [closed] <short title>``; other closed items are omitted; and an index with no unchecked or retained checked entries contains exactly `- None.`. The status and title are byte-identical to the item heading and the index uses no table. The current-snapshot check proves every unresolved item is listed and every retained checked line names a closed item; whether a checked historical line was improperly deleted is self-reported because a stateless corpus validator cannot reconstruct prior membership.

`scripts/validate-doc-model.mjs` enforces only this current-snapshot `findings.md` structure through the existing documentation-model check: document and heading grammar, status and ID vocabulary, ID uniqueness, exact field syntax, fixed field names and order, status-specific closing fields, and current Follow-up Index agreement. It does not decide whether a statement, owner, status, verdict, or evidence claim is true or sufficient, and it cannot prove historical index retention. It adds no schema file or automatic writer and does not validate `route-log.md`, whose unchecked one-line contract above remains separate.

Noisy transcripts, raw tool output, timing, temporary checkpoints, and intermediate evidence stay uncommitted under `temp/`. Temporary material may be lost at any time, so its value is harvested into committed homes when work is parked, at checkpoints and closeout, and at an Auditor triage: an open question with an owner goes to that owner's open-questions or deferred section, material bound to a change goes to that change record's findings and proposal, and a decision goes to a decision record, while material with neither owner nor change keeps only its status header. Every temporary work package opens with a status header of at most ten lines naming its status (active, parked, harvested, or abandoned), the question it answers, settled items with their decision records, open questions, the next step, the reopen condition when parked, the related change record, and the last update date. Harvested text is public, so host details, credentials, private transcripts, and unverified inference are removed first. Deleting temporary material remains a data-loss decision under the Safety Kernel. Retain enough raw material for the three approved pilot plans to support later task-external review, but do not install a new event schema, workflow engine, dashboard, or automatic blocker before repeated evidence demonstrates that need. The decision and its reason are recorded in [a decision record](decisions/20260923-temp_is_disposable.md).

A plan's uncommitted material mirrors its committed bundle: `temp/changes/` holds one directory per plan under the same name as that plan's `docs/changes/` bundle, and that directory holds its scripts, probes, attempt evidence, and raw output. A plan may still reference and use other `temp/` paths where the work needs them.

At closeout, summarize what changed, link the accepted owners and commits, report exact verification, record unresolved findings, and set the lifecycle status accurately. Record a few raw complexity numbers for the change: files changed, owning documents changed, distinct owners touched, and any closed set or registry added. At a release boundary or on request an Auditor compares these numbers as trends; they explain and are never builder targets, thresholds, or gates. The Auditor routes essential complexity, the price of an engineer's requirement, to the engineer as a trade-off, and accidental complexity to a finding with its owner and smallest correction; a correction that changes governing design follows the challenge path above. After a correction the same signal is measured again and its raw change reported, and a signal that recurs after correction goes to the engineer as a structural question. Promote durable decisions and rationale to their actual owners. Change records are removable evidence and may be pruned after no active authority or plan depends on them. The decision and its reason are recorded in [a decision record](decisions/20260923-system_complexity_signals.md).

## Maintenance Rounds

Besides design upkeep inside each change, a concentrated maintenance round removes accumulated residue. Signals rather than a calendar start one: complexity trends at an audit, the number and age of deferred findings, recurrence of one defect class, an agent reimplementing an existing capability, observed change amplification, a release boundary, a completed governance change, or growing unharvested temporary material. A round changes no behavior: it commits behavior-preserving changes apart from any behavior change, writes characterization tests before touching untested code, registers each hypothesis and expected observable before editing, works in small reversible slices, compares baseline and candidate with the same oracle, and obtains independent acceptance. Each deletion candidate ends in one of the removal outcomes defined by `docs/specs/20260719-verification_calibration.md`, with mechanisms that back each other up analyzed together. An Auditor evaluates the round from raw before and after observations, registered predictions against results, and later regressions attributable to it, without scores or line-count targets. Over-design is watched by the Consultant at design time, the reviewer at each diff, and the Auditor across accumulated work, including documents. The decision and its reason are recorded in [a decision record](decisions/20260924-periodic_maintenance_rounds.md).

## Commits And External Effects

Commit only with user authorization. Follow `CONTRIBUTING.md`; keep related changes coherent and use dependency order when independently versioned packages require it. Preserve unrelated user changes and do not amend, rewrite, push, publish, deploy, contact external systems, use credentials, or perform destructive cleanup unless the task and authorization cover that exact effect.

Real-environment work names the target, prerequisites, cleanup, retained evidence, and residual state before the effect. A failed attempt may still be useful evidence when it names the missing fact and proves cleanup and unrelated-state preservation, but it never becomes a product PASS by narration.

## Completion

Material work closes only when direct evidence settles the recorded acceptance observations, actual artifacts align with their owners, applicable safety and cleanup obligations are satisfied, and unresolved findings are honestly retained. The primary agent reports the diff, commands, exact results, external effects, and remaining uncertainty.

The engineer decides changes to Intent, architecture, governing trade-offs, strict-risk acceptance, and important residual risk, and remains available when a viable path cannot be found. Otherwise the primary continues, reframes, or closes ordinary work under existing authorization.
